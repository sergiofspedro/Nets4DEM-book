## Theme 1: Ecosystem vs. Network vs. Alliance — The Strategic Choice of Organisational Form

**Theme summary:** Luca Miggiano explicitly distinguishes Ecosistema Futuro's organisational form from traditional networks and alliances. He describes the ecosystem as "like the mycelium of mushrooms... something without a center... more scattered, that simply grows." This contrasts with ASVIS itself, which is a more traditional alliance with "a very large secretariat, 40 people, there are members who are organized into working groups... a slightly more traditional form." The ecosystem is aspirational — "I don't know if we'll succeed because it's very complex" — but represents a deliberate attempt to create a more distributed, non-hierarchical mode of collaboration where "all the participants can use the Ecosistema Futuro logo without asking my permission" (unlike ASVIS patronage, which requires formal approval).

**Detailed explanation:** Luca traces the conceptual genealogy of the ecosystem model. Enrico Giovannini (ASVIS founder) had a clear idea derived from alliance experience but wanted Ecosistema Futuro to go further. The ecosystem has a central team (2 people, soon 3) but it is "not a large secretariat." Task forces are led by participants themselves: "our task forces are actually made up of the participants, they also coordinate them." The knowledge exchange days (November 2025) are designed without frontal presentations — instead, they put participants in contact laterally. The open-use logo policy enacts distributed ownership: "if they say tomorrow let's set this up, in fact I encourage them so they are truly part of the ecosystem." However, Luca acknowledges the practical tension: "people come to you asking, 'Ah, but how do you do this thing?' In any case, you always have a role as responsible."

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: ASVIS (parent organization), Ecosistema Futuro, Enrico Giovannini, Luca Miggiano, Steering Committee (~20 people).
- Practices: Open-use logo policy (no permission required), task forces led by participants, knowledge exchange days, email-based membership acceptance.
- Contradiction: Aspiration toward distributed, non-hierarchical form vs. practical need for coordination and responsibility.

## Theme 2: Light Governance Through Logo Policy and Steering Committee

**Theme summary:** Ecosistema Futuro's governance is deliberately light. Membership is by invitation from the Steering Committee, accepted informally via email (or formal MoU for public institutions like universities). The primary governance lever is the logo usage policy: the Steering Committee "reserves the right to remove the use of the logo" if a participant misuses it. Otherwise, "all the participants can use the Ecosistema Futuro logo without asking my permission." This creates a governance model where the only sanction is exclusion from the shared identity, and the primary obligation is alignment with the strategy document and logo usage rules.

**Detailed explanation:** The governance structure is triangular: a Steering Committee (~20 founding institutions and strategic partners, chaired by Giovannini) makes strategic decisions (strategy, business plan, membership invitations); ASVIS provides legal entity status and manages criminal/legal responsibilities, with the Secretary General as Luca's line manager; and Luca's small team provides operational coordination. The Steering Committee decides three things: strategy, business plan, and invitations to join. Gender equity rules apply — organizations must name a delegate and an alternate, with the alternate of the opposite sex. For universities and public institutions, formal MoUs (about 35 of ~100 participants) create legally binding relationships, including dispute resolution clauses. No conflicts have emerged so far, partly because "the topic is still very little politicized about the future" (IP05, Luca Miggiano).

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: Steering Committee (chaired by Enrico Giovannini), Luca Miggiano, ASVIS Secretary General, ~100 participating organizations (35 with formal MoU).
- Practices: Logo usage policy as primary governance tool; email-based membership; gender equity rules for delegates; MoU for public institutions.
- Problems: Governance not yet stress-tested by reputational crises.

**Key quotations:** *"All the participants can use the Ecosistema Futuro logo without asking my permission, while for ASVIS patronage you need permission."* (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

## Theme 3: SOIF/SUAF as International Peer Network — Light, Informal, Inspirational

The relationship is sustained by personal conviction, not organisational mandate: Luca explicitly carved out time for international networking despite it not being in Ecosistema Futuro's formal outcome framework.

**Detailed explanation:** The Dubai Future Forum functions as an unstructured filtering space where Luca identifies the "serious" futures practitioners among the "many charlatans." Financial arrangements are variable: sometimes Luca self-finances participation, sometimes SOIF pays. ## Theme 4: Strategic Non-Politicization of "The Future"

**Theme summary:** A key strategic insight: the topic of "the future" is not yet politicized in Italy, which allows Ecosistema Futuro to build broad coalitions across political divides. The constitutional reform on future generations (2022) passed almost unanimously, and the generational impact assessment law was pushed by a centre-right politician (Casellati). Luca is uncertain whether this non-politicization is durable: *"I don't know what the answer is... this is a topic that hasn't been politicized yet and so people still use it a bit as common sense."* Some critics argue that if it passed unanimously, "it means it is not relevant." Luca leans toward the first interpretation — that it represents a genuine, pre-political consensus — but acknowledges the uncertainty.

**Detailed explanation:** Luca identifies a paradox: the very factors that enable Ecosistema Futuro's broad coalition (the topic's non-politicization) may also limit its transformative potential (if it remains non-contentious, it may lack the political energy to drive change). The ecosystem's deliberate positioning as "mainstream" — "the most reformist of the mainstream" — facilitates access to mainstream institutions and funders but may limit engagement with more radical futures discourse (non-human rights, post-collapse governance, radical ecological perspectives). Luca acknowledges this tension: "we are not, let's say, in the economy... new economy." The ecosystem deals with "econometricians, economists who tell you if it is not quantifiable, it does not exist."

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: Enrico Giovannini, Casellati (centre-right politician), constitutional reform (2022), generational impact assessment law (2024).
- Practices: Mainstream positioning; pre-political consensus-building.
- Contradiction: Non-politicization enables coalition-building but may limit transformative ambition.

**Key quotations:** *"This is a topic that hasn't been politicized yet and so people still use it a bit as common sense."* (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

## Theme 5: The National Assembly on the Future as Democratic Experiment

**Theme summary:** Ecosistema Futuro's most ambitious current project is a National Assembly on the Future — a civic assembly of 100–150 people that will discuss Italy's future. A constituent assembly of 52 people (40 under 35, 12 mentors including former prime ministers Giuliano Amato and Mario Monti, former Constitutional Court president Silvana Sciarra) is designing the process. This represents a significant evolution — from research, advocacy, and awareness-raising to direct democratic experimentation.

**Detailed explanation:** The Assembly is framed as potentially unique: "the civic assembly on the future... could be almost unique in the world, because there was one in Norway, but the others are on climate." It represents Ecosistema Futuro moving from being a knowledge producer and network facilitator to being a democratic innovator. The project involves multiple components: the constituent assembly, an online consultation (5,000–6,000 people, demographically stratified), and "Piazza sul futuro" (participatory events for 80–100 young people in locations distributed across Italy: Agnone/Molise, Catanzaro, Milan, Parma). Luca explicitly seeks legitimacy through connection with established experts in civic assemblies (mentioning Luigi Di Marco and Giovanni Allegretti).

**Specific elements:**
- Projects: National Assembly on the Future (forthcoming), constituent assembly (52 people), online consultation (5,000–6,000), Piazza sul futuro (participatory events).
- Individuals: Giuliano Amato, Silvana Sciarra, Mario Monti, Gianfranco Fini, Luigi Di Marco.
- Territories: Agnone (Molise), Catanzaro, Milan, Parma.
- Opportunities: Unique positioning as a futures-focused civic assembly.

**Key quotations:** *"This civic assembly on the future, I hope, if we do it well, could be almost unique in the world."* (IP05, Luca Miggiano)
