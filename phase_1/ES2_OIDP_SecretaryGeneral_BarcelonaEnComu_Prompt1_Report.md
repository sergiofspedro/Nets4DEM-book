## Theme 2: The Paradox of Success — Bureaucratisation and the Costs of Institutionalisation

### Summary
The very transparency and accountability reforms that have improved public administration in Catalonia have paradoxically made it harder for city networks to function. The speaker describes how the evolution of public procurement doctrine, auditing requirements, and transparency policies — while welcome in principle — have "hindered networking and economic contributions to city networks."

### Detailed Explanation
The speaker observes a significant shift during their tenure. "What used to happen was that a chapter would be opened within the budget, the invoice would be added there, and that was it. It was very simple. Now it's often a bureaucratic nightmare because it has to be in the budget, it has to be itemized."

The costs of transparency and accountability — in terms of administrative burden — have made municipalities more cautious about contributing to networks. "The very evolution of public procurement doctrine, of administrative procedure, the impact that all the transparency policies have had, which are welcome, but which are hindering networking and economic contributions to city networks."

This creates a paradox: the same values of openness and accountability that networks like OIDP promote in the public sphere are, when applied to the networks themselves, creating barriers to their operation. The speaker notes that contributing to a network is now "questioned by the legal services, the auditing department, and the secretariat" — the very guardians of public integrity.

## Theme 3: "Comet Networks" — The Ephemeral Nature of Politically-Led Networks

### Summary
The speaker distinguishes between two types of city networks: institutionalised networks with legal status (like OIDP) that survive political changes, and "comet networks" created by political projects that burn brightly and disappear when the government changes. The Barcelona en Comú administration created "hundreds" of such networks, but most have already vanished.

### Detailed Explanation
The speaker describes the intense period of the Barcelona en Comú administration (2015–2023) as a time of "intense political activity" in which the city created networks on almost every issue: city refuge networks, anti-overtourism networks, networks for public energy, for public water. "We created hundreds of networks because it was a time of intense political activity."

The key insight: "networks that have endured are those that have become institutionalised as associations." The Association of Municipalities for Public Energy and the Association of Municipalities for Public Water survive because they have legal status, membership structures, and rotating presidencies. The informal networks — refugee cities, tourism management — "no longer exist" because they were tied to "a specific electoral cycle and personal leadership."

Giovanni calls these "comet networks" — "they come through, are very important for a while, and then disappear." The speaker agrees: these networks are "more closely linked to a specific electoral cycle and personal leadership. Often, to the person who establishes them."

The lesson is that institutionalisation — creating a legal entity, a membership structure, a rotating presidency — is necessary for network survival. But institutionalisation also creates the rigidities and bureaucratic costs that the speaker described earlier.
