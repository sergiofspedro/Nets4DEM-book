## Theme 1: Decidim and People Powered as Fundamentally Different Organisational Models

**Theme summary:** Carla offers the most analytically precise comparative assessment of Decidim and People Powered in the entire interview corpus. She characterises Decidim as having "a very defined political view" rooted in the 15M movement in Spain — "they do not see technology as simply a tool... they do have a very defined political view of how to use technology." People Powered, by contrast, is "politically and financially completely different" — founded as a hub with much more diverse political perspectives, "much more diverse... to a point that it's hard even to agree on what's democracy." Both models have strengths and limitations: Decidim's political clarity may restrict its appeal to certain governments or funders; People Powered's openness may create "limitations as well for political action."

**Detailed explanation:** Carla traces Decidim's political origins to the 15M demonstrations (2011) and the political movements they generated (Podemos, Barcelona en Comú). The platform's founders are described as "politically forged" in that moment, with a strong philosophical view that technology is not neutral. This political clarity is simultaneously a strength (ethical coherence, clear value proposition) and a limitation (may restrict adoption by governments with different orientations, though Barcelona's transition from Comú to PSOE demonstrated resilience). People Powered was founded from a different position — as a hub of scholars and practitioners with diverse perspectives, strong at securing American-style philanthropic funding, and deliberately avoiding direct connection to governments. Carla notes: "I heard Josh saying one time that People Powered also was created to do what IOPD was not doing — you already have a network of governments, we don't want to do that, we want to strengthen civil society." The contrast illuminates different theories of change: political commitment vs. broad-tent pluralism.

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: Decidim founders (philosophers, political philosophers, Barcelona en Comú), People Powered (Josh Learner), OIDP, 15M movement, Podemos.
- Events: 15M demonstrations (2011), rise of Barcelona en Comú and Podemos.
- Contradiction: Political clarity expands and restricts; openness includes and dilutes.

## Theme 2: Open Source Resilience and the Decidim Ecosystem

**Theme summary:** Carla describes how Decidim's open-source community — a network of local governments using the technology and specialised enterprises developing it — provides resilience that proprietary or single-developer platforms lack. Her Brazilian government experience illustrates this: previous government platforms built on a single developer's technology (Nosferu) collapsed when federal funding was cut. The Decidim ecosystem, with its diverse implementers (enterprises, governments, developers), creates redundancy and continuity. "We were very much concerned about not joining a technology that would not have a community that would continuously develop it." The modularity of Decidim was also critical — compared to Consul, which lacked modularity and thus limited adaptability.

**Detailed explanation:** Carla's analysis is grounded in her experience as Director of Digital Participation for Brasil Participativo, which became "the bigger installation of Decidim around the world" (1.5 million of Decidim's 3 million total users). The selection of Decidim for Brazil's federal participation platform was based on three factors: (a) the ecosystem's resilience, demonstrated by Barcelona's continuity across political changes (unlike Decidim Madrid/Consul); (b) the modularity that allowed customisation for Brazil's specific needs (mobile-first design, simplified interface, reduced information density); and (c) the political alignment between Decidim's open-source ethics and Brazil's participatory democracy tradition. The contrast with the Nosferu experience — where a local cooperative's technology died with federal funding — was decisive.

**Specific elements:**
- Projects: Brasil Participativo (federal platform), Partipa.br (predecessor, failed), Nosferu (failed technology).
- Actors: Brazilian federal government, University of Brasília (LAPIS lab), Barcelona City Council, Generalitat de Catalunya, Open Source Politics, Decidim enterprises.
- Problems: Single-developer dependency vs. ecosystem resilience.

**Key quotations:** *"We had had previous experiences... it was built in a technology called Nosferu... they had no real ecosystem that provided resilience... when the federal government discontinued it, the software ended up."* (IPO4, Carla Bezerra)

## Theme 3: Funding Diversification as the Key Resilience Strategy

**Theme summary:** Carla identifies funding diversification as the central resilience strategy for both Decidim and People Powered, though they approach it differently. Decidim "still had more than 50 percent dependent on the Ayuntamiento de Barcelona" but is "trying very much to diversify." People Powered, by contrast, "has proved to be extremely resilient" through its strategy of "incorporating Europe to be able to apply to European grants, to be able to not be dependent on one specific funder." However, Carla notes a tension: many People Powered programmes "are done because they are... a condition of the funder" — they align with general goals but are "very much formatted by the donors."

**Detailed explanation:** Carla provides the most detailed financial analysis of the two networks. Decidim's dependency on Barcelona (city + regional government) has survived political changes (Comú → PSOE) but leaves the project exposed to a single-funder risk. Diversification efforts include building an international user base (Brazil, France, Japan) that can contribute to the ecosystem's sustainability. People Powered's model is American-style philanthropic fundraising — diversified across Open Society, USAID (partially cut under Trump), and now European grants. The cost of this model is programme shaping by donors: "the climate democracy accelerators, or the narratives alliance, they are interesting projects, they are very much aligned with the goals of people powered, but they are also very much formatted... by the donors." Both approaches represent different answers to the same question: how to maintain mission integrity while securing resources.

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: Barcelona City Council (Decidim primary funder), Generalitat de Catalunya, Open Society, USAID, European grants.
- Practices: Diversification through international user base (Decidim); diversification through multi-donor fundraising (People Powered).
- Contradiction: Donor funding brings resources but shapes programmes; single-funder dependency brings stability but creates vulnerability.

**Key quotations:** *"People Powered has proved to be extremely resilient... the strategy of incorporating Europe to be able to apply to European grants, to be able to not be dependent on one specific funder."* (IPO4, Carla Bezerra)

This illustrates the paradox: institutional position opens doors, but personal expertise and relationships sustain network engagement beyond institutional roles.
