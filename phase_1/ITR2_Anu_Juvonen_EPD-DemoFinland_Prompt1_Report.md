## Theme 1: EPD's Three-Pillar Structure — Advocacy, Programming, Research

**Theme summary:** Anu describes EPD's evolution from a primarily programme-focused network (EU calls, project content) to a three-pillar structure: advocacy (collective voice in Brussels), programming (EU-funded projects), and research (the Democracy Hub, created with Carnegie Endowment). The advocacy function was present from the start because many member organisations lacked Brussels representation. The research pillar was added later: "we felt that there is a gap in our work... that's to provide evidence-based information for decision makers and for us members." The Democracy Hub is described as "a success" — it produced a China report (with Carnegie) on China's soft power in the Global South.

**Detailed explanation:** EPD has grown to 21 members and receives constant membership applications. Anu frames the organisation's strength as residing in its "diversity of members" — both geographical and thematic: "we're trying to balance a geographical footprint but also the thematic focusses that our membership is covering the variety of aspects on democracy support." As President of the Board, she introduced an annual board retreat (first in August 2024) to create space for strategic reflection: "we also felt and I felt it very strongly that we have such great minds in the board."

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: EPD (21 members), Carnegie Endowment, DEMO Finland, NIMD (Netherlands Institute for Multiparty Democracy).
- Projects: Democracy Hub, China soft power report (January 2026).
- Practices: Three-pillar structure; board retreat; membership diversity balancing.
- Territories: Brussels (advocacy), Europe, Global South (China report focus).

**Key quotations:** *"We have advocacy, we have the programming and then research and it's been a success."* (ITR2, Anu Juvonen)
