## Theme 1: The Multi-Tenant Gap — Why Italian Municipalities Cannot Afford Decidim

**Theme summary:** Giulia identifies the absence of multi-tenant Decidim infrastructure in Italy as the single greatest barrier to adoption by small municipalities. A multi-tenant architecture — where a regional authority hosts Decidim centrally and provides access to multiple municipalities — would dramatically reduce per-municipality costs. Catalonia has implemented this via AOC; Italy has not. Giulia has repeatedly advocated for it with the Emilia-Romagna region without success: *"I really don't understand... what the reason is that Emilia Romagna can't make the leap."* The result is a structural exclusion of small municipalities from the Decidim ecosystem — they want the platform but cannot afford individual installations. PARES started doing "garage" installations but "we finally found ourselves unable to provide it to them at prices they can afford."

**Detailed explanation:** The multi-tenant problem is fundamentally a governance problem, not a technical one. The technology exists (Catalonia proves it), but regional authorities lack the political will to implement it. Giulia describes municipalities calling to ask "When is it coming? Because we need this stuff." The Emilia-Romagna region, despite being advanced in participatory practice, uses Decidim primarily as a transparency platform for funded projects rather than enabling genuine participatory processes. The interviewer notes that multi-tenant governance "has the ability to guarantee both centralization and autonomy... you can collect all the data... grant complete autonomy to the peripheries, but maintain a constant center." The absence of this infrastructure means that the participatory potential of Decidim in Italy is systematically under-realised.

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: PARES, Emilia-Romagna Region, Municipality of Reggio Emilia, AOC (Catalonia), Michele D'Alena.
- Practices: Multi-tenant architecture; garage installations vs. professional hosting.
- Problems: Political will gap; cost barrier for small municipalities (~50,000 small municipalities in Italy).
- Territories: Italy (Emilia-Romagna, Sardinia), Catalonia (AOC model).

**Key quotations:** *"A municipality called us... to say, 'When is it coming? Because we need this stuff. We can't use the space currently guaranteed by the regional platform.'"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)

## Theme 2: The Tool-Culture Gap — Administrations Buy the Platform Without the Participation

**Theme summary:** Giulia describes a persistent gap between technological capability and administrative imagination. Administrations acquire Decidim but lack the participatory culture to use it meaningfully. *"They often ask us, 'Do we want the platform, do we want to set it up?' It's a bit like the idea that the tool alone — boom! — will create who knows what."* The platform is used "only as a questionnaire about a policy you're developing" — far below its potential for complex deliberative processes. The national-level deployment through Formez (Italian government agency) was particularly disappointing: "there was a lot of celebration when 50 questionnaires were filled out nationwide, but what are we talking about?"

**Detailed explanation:** Giulia describes a pattern where the tool is acquired but not the cultural transformation needed to use it effectively. Administrations see the platform as a solution rather than infrastructure. At the national level (Formez/Department of Civil Service), the experience was frustrating: "inside there, with the rubber walls... technology is the least of the problems." The administration's internal complexity, slow decision-making, and lack of participatory culture made the tool ineffective. Yet the national adoption had a legitimizing effect: "The fact that a central administration... has focused on the platform... seemed to have legitimized and emphasized the importance of tools like these."

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: Formez (Italian government agency), Department of Civil Service, Italian municipalities.
- Practices: Training as cultural translation (included in service packages); national-level legitimization; low-ambition platform usage.
- Problems: Tool acquired without participatory culture; administrative complexity at national level.

**Key quotations:** *"They often ask us, 'Do we want the platform, do we want to set it up?' It's a bit like the idea that the tool alone — boom! — will create who knows what."* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)

## Theme 3: Parma as Counter-Example — When the Platform Becomes Participatory Infrastructure

**Theme summary:** The Municipality of Parma provides a success case. PARES supported Parma in rewriting its participation regulations and integrating Decidim with neighborhood workshops (formerly citizen committees from the 1950s). The platform became infrastructure for an ongoing participatory governance process: *"A mechanism has been triggered for these workshops to function, one that communicates closely with the platform... It has contributed not only to transparency, but also to ordering communication flows."* Proposals are uploaded, discussed in person, evaluated by the Participation Office, and receive public responses — creating an institutionalized feedback loop between citizens and administration.

**Detailed explanation:** Parma demonstrates what Decidim can achieve when combined with political will and institutional design. The neighborhood workshops (Consigli di Quartiere transformed into Laboratori di Quartiere) use the platform as a shared workspace — proposals, comments, and administrative responses are all tracked through Decidim. This created order where previously there was "a mess of relationships" between workshop members calling councilors directly. The platform enabled "the administration to take institutional ownership of this proposal at the city level, outside of the channels that it is always best to interrupt." This case illustrates that technology alone does not create participation — but when embedded in well-designed institutional processes, it can transform how participation functions.

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: Municipality of Parma, PARES, neighborhood workshops (Laboratori di Quartiere), Participation Office.
- Practices: Platform-integrated neighborhood workshops; public administrative responses to citizen proposals.
- Territories: Parma (Italy).

**Key quotations:** *"A mechanism has been triggered for these workshops to function, one that communicates closely with the platform... It has contributed not only to transparency, but also to ordering communication flows."* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)

## Theme 4: Asymmetric Community Participation — Time Poverty as Governance Barrier

**Theme summary:** Giulia acknowledges that PARES does not actively participate in MetaDecidim's development governance, despite being an official partner. She follows discussions but doesn't contribute to priority-setting: *"I see things happening... but unfortunately I haven't had time to actively participate yet."* The time required is minimal — *"I realized that very little was needed"* — but even that is beyond the capacity of a small enterprise. This creates a structural bias: those with more resources (larger enterprises, well-funded institutions) have disproportionate influence on the community's development priorities. Governments and end-users have no formal seats in MetaDecidim governance.

**Detailed explanation:** This theme reveals a democratic deficit in the Decidim community's governance. The community that governs the software's development is primarily composed of those with time and technical expertise — developers and larger enterprises. Small consultancies like PARES, and especially the public administrations they serve, have limited voice in setting development priorities. Giulia observes but cannot assess whether the governance processes are genuinely democratic: *"I can't tell you whether this participation in the design and governance of the software project is real or just a facade."* This uncertainty itself is significant — peripheral community members lack visibility into whether governance processes are substantive or performative. Yet the community's culture is genuinely welcoming: at Decidim Fest in Barcelona, *"there was a great openness, even to new people... a great willingness and welcome."*

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: PARES, MetaDecidim community, Decidim Association, other Decidim enterprises.
- Practices: Proposal collection for development priorities; Decidim Fest as knowledge diffusion; asymmetric community governance participation.
- Problems: Time poverty creates governance bias; developer-centric governance excludes end-users.

**Key quotations:** *"I can't tell you whether this participation in the design and governance of the software project is real or just a facade."* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)
