## Theme 1: ALDA as an Association with a Networking Dimension

**Theme summary:** Antonella describes ALDA's distinctive model: it was founded as a formal association (legal status) but always with a strong networking dimension. The interviewer frames this as a key analytical question: "We are interviewing networks which are just networks and wanted to remain informal, so they don't have funding facilities. Starting from your start, how you inverted, you started as an association formally, but with the network dimension." This places ALDA in the Nets4DEM typology as an association-network hybrid — a legal entity that embeds networking practices within its formal structure, giving it access to funding mechanisms that purely informal networks lack.

**Detailed explanation:** ALDA was created in the context of the Council of Europe's local democracy agencies programme in the Western Balkans. The choice of the association legal form was strategic: it enabled ALDA to access EU funding, manage grants, and employ staff — functions that informal networks cannot perform. But ALDA's operational logic remained networked: connecting local authorities, civil society organisations, and citizens across Europe. The interviewer explicitly seeks to understand this as a "pattern of networking" distinct from the informal network model (KNOCA) or the ecosystem model (Ecosistema Futuro).

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: ALDA, Council of Europe, local democracy agencies (Balkans).
- Legal form: Association (registered legal entity).
- Territories: Western Balkans (origin), France/Italy (base), Europe.
- Practices: Association-network hybrid; networking as embedded within formal structure.

**Key quotations:** *"Starting from your start, how you inverted, you started as an association formally, but with the network dimension."* (Interviewer, IFP1 context)
