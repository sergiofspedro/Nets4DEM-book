Giovanni identifies this as a distinctive feature of product-centred networks: they must "balance contributions to the object with contributions to the debate surrounding the object." Unlike purely idea-driven networks, Decidim has a physical artefact (the code) that creates a concrete metric of contribution.

## Theme 3: The Dilemma of Working with WhatsApp in Mozambique — Ethics vs. Pragmatism

### Summary
A concrete ethical dilemma arises when Platonic is asked to implement WhatsApp integration for a client in Mozambique, where internet access is expensive and limited. The speaker defends the choice pragmatically: enabling participation through WhatsApp lowers the barrier for citizens who cannot afford internet access. This pragmatic response to Global South constraints is contrasted with the more purist approach of European actors who criticised the solution.

### Detailed Explanation
Giovanni raises the ethical question: should a company that values democratic participation work with a platform like WhatsApp (owned by Meta), which has its own problematic record on privacy and democracy? The speaker's response is direct and practical. In Mozambique, citizens cannot afford internet access but have WhatsApp included in their mobile plans. "WhatsApp [is] a less expensive way for citizens to contribute to the platform. Those with internet access can use it directly there, but if you don't have an institution and internet is expensive, add[ing] by WhatsApp is much cheaper."

The speaker criticises Octree for having presented a WhatsApp integration at Decidim Fest but not launching it because "they weren't sure it would work" — making a video instead of a working product. Platonic's approach is different: they build functional connectors ("WhatsApp, Telegram, something else") and adapt to the technological reality of their clients.

This reveals a split between European technical purism and the practical needs of the Global South. The speaker sides with pragmatism: if the goal is participation, the means must be accessible, even if imperfect.

- **Criticism of Octree**: Presented non-functional video, never launched

The tension reveals a deeper structural question: who pays for participatory infrastructure? The public sector provides basic access (multi-tenant), while private companies offer enhanced services. The speaker does not resolve this tension but lives with it pragmatically.
