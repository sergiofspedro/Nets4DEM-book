## Theme 1: Two Phases of People Powered — From Intimate Community to Scaled Network

**Theme summary:** Andres describes two distinct phases of his engagement with People Powered. In the first years (2019–2023), the network was small and intimate — "very easy as a member to get involved," with a "direct connection between the conversations you were involved in, and the outcome of those conversations." Andres could see his contribution in what People Powered produced: tools, resources, methodological approaches, the Resource Center. After the Mexico convening, People Powered adopted a growth strategy and expanded significantly — "it grows a lot, and that is great because you're reaching a larger community," but "this proximity with the members kind of loses, it becomes less personal, less direct, this feedback to your engagement."

**Detailed explanation:** Andres traces his entry into People Powered through the OIDP conference in Barcelona (2019), which included a Hewlett Foundation workshop that catalysed the transition from the PB Hub to People Powered. His organisation, Coglobal, joined in summer 2019. In the early phase, he was personally involved in "internal debates... strategic and global views" and specific projects (research on participatory budgeting's educational effects, published in an academic journal). He felt a strong connection between his contributions and People Powered's outputs. The growth phase after Mexico brought increased global influence — "everybody in our world knows about People Powered" — but diluted the personal engagement. This tension between scale and intimacy is a recurring theme. After 2023, Coglobal entered a more structured partnership with People Powered through the Nets4Dem project, with joint staff (Alisa and Juanfran), creating an organic but complex relationship: "a lot of exciting opportunities, but also brings along different challenges, just like harmonizing our different goals."

## Theme 2: People Power Decides — Participatory Governance with Limitations

**Theme summary:** Andres values People Powered's pioneering internal democracy — "People Power Decides" is essentially a participatory budgeting process for setting yearly priorities. He notes: "I think we can agree that in most networks it's not common that you have participatory processes such as People Power decides." However, he identifies limitations: there is not "a strong horizontal communication between members," and the process can feel "a little bit aggregative" without "collective deliberation around which priorities should be the ones that end up in the agenda." The biennial/triennial convenings (Warsaw, Mexico, Nairobi) partially compensate, but budget and resource constraints limit participation.

**Detailed explanation:** Andres appreciates the ambition of applying participatory methods internally — "we're all a little bit experts in participatory approaches. So we can see the benefit. We can also see certain shortcomings." The main shortcoming is that members without existing connections cannot deliberate together before voting, making the process aggregative rather than deliberative. The convenings create space for deeper conversation, but "not everybody can be there." He frames this as a normal stage in organisational development: "once you achieve a goal, you see the problems or the contradictions that you have to face once you are on that next stage."

**Specific elements:**
- Practices: People Power Decides (participatory priority-setting), biennial/triennial convenings.
- Actors: Members from Africa, Europe, Latin America.
- Problems: Horizontal communication gaps, aggregative vs. deliberative decision-making.

**Key quotations:** *"In most networks it's not common that you have participatory processes such as People Power decides. So I think that is a big leap forward."*

## Theme 3: Asymmetry Between Paid Staff and Volunteer Members

**Theme summary:** Andres identifies a structural tension between People Powered's paid staff (~15 people) and its member organisations (many with 1 paid person and volunteers): "There is an asymmetry in the sense that... your capacity of following up... is obviously difficult, so on some level you are dependent on what the people power staff, like small decisions that they make every day." He also notes that staff salaries are adjusted by cost of living (a Brazil-based staff member earns less than a New York-based one), while honoraria for punctual work (mentorship) are equal regardless of location — creating an internal inconsistency.

**Detailed explanation:** The asymmetry exists simply because paid staff have more time to devote to network activities than members who must prioritise their own organisations' work. This creates a structural dependency of members on staff decisions. The salary differential between Global North and South staff is a "really complicated issue" with no clear answer — Andres notes the development aid principle of adjusting salaries to local cost of living to avoid creating extreme wealth disparities, but acknowledges the ethical complexity. The honoraria policy (equal pay for equal work regardless of location) was a topic of discussion at the Nairobi convening.

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: People Powered staff (~15), member organisations, mentors.
- Practices: Location-adjusted staff salaries, equal honoraria for mentorship work.
- Problems: Staff-member asymmetry; salary equity across Global North/South.

**Key quotations:** *"If the staff was paid by contributions of the members and not by external funding, then you could theoretically have a pure connection between the priorities decided by the members and what the staff is doing."*

## Theme 4: Decidim Critique — Complex for Small Municipalities

**Theme summary:** Andres provides a critical assessment of Decidim from the perspective of a small-organisation practitioner in Andalusia. He finds Decidim "too much of a machine" for small municipalities — "it's like you're going to buy your groceries at a Ferrari." The platform is "not intuitive... not user-friendly" and "not particularly transparent" outside active process phases. His organisation, Coglobal, developed its own simpler platform designed for small municipalities with limited staff and technical capacity.

**Detailed explanation:** Andres's critique is grounded in the realities of Andalusian local governments, where "local governments have a very low budget, they normally don't have any staff that is dedicated specifically to participation." Decidim's interface complexity is a barrier: Coglobal created "our own platform... software as a service that is specifically designed for small municipalities, for transparency purposes and voting purposes." It is "not versatile at all" but "very user friendly." He also critiques the landing pages of Decidim implementations: "nine out of ten times, it's challenging" for citizens who "didn't even want to participate. They just want to vote for... Port St. Escort in their neighborhood."

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: Coglobal, Decidim, Andalusian municipalities.
- Technologies: Coglobal's own platform (software as a service), Decidim, Consul.
- Territories: Andalusia, Catalonia (AOC multi-tenant).
- Problems: Decidim's complexity as a barrier for small municipalities; lack of intuitive citizen interfaces.

**Key quotations:** *"Decidim for small municipalities is just too much of a machine. So it's like you're going to buy your groceries at a Ferrari."*

## Theme 5: The Growth Dilemma — How to Scale Without Losing Connection

**Theme summary:** Andres articulates a fundamental dilemma for membership networks: growth increases reach and influence, but dilutes the personal connection between members and the network. He is "not very optimistic about it, but not in the sense that I think it's going bad, just that I can't really foresee a scenario in which that would be working very well." The risk is that People Powered becomes like "Greenpeace or Amnesty International" — "getting a newsletter once a month... a letter of gratifying, like appreciating our contribution." This is not a critique of People Powered's management but an observation about the inherent tension between scale and intimacy.

**Detailed explanation:** Andres reflects on what members actually receive from network participation. In the early phase, the value was tangible: tools, resources, methodological improvements for Coglobal's work. As People Powered scaled, many outputs became less directly connected to member needs — "a couple of people from the People Powered staff meeting with people from other organizations... and then you get delivered a guide... but it's hard to see the thread from your initial needs." The question of what members receive — "what am I receiving from this network? What am I getting for my staff, for my relationship, for my connections?" — becomes harder to answer at scale.

**Specific elements:**
- Practices: Resource Center, guides/manuals, democratic narratives alliance.
- Contradiction: Scale increases influence but erodes member value perception.

**Key quotations:** *"How can you grow to certain extents and at the same time have a meaningful relationship with the people that are involved in the network? I think that's a challenge."*
