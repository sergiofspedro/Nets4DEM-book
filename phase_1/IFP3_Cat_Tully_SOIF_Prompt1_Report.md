## Theme 3: The Prism — Futures + Participation + Accountability

**Theme summary:** SOIF's analytical framework combines three elements: (1) futures and foresight; (2) participatory and deliberative processes bringing peripheral voices into decision-making; (3) accountability mechanisms (intergenerational fairness assessments, parliamentary committees of the future). Cat argues futures work uniquely justifies participation on epistemic grounds: "it's not just about legitimacy... It's actually because [people at the periphery] are better at giving you quality insights about how the future might emerge." Representative democracy "fails to represent a key stakeholder, which is future generations."

**Detailed explanation:** The prism is SOIF's answer to institutionalising long-term thinking. Futures provides analytical capacity. Participation ensures those who will live in the future have voice. Accountability prevents leaders from defecting to short-termism. Cat warns that citizens' assemblies risk replicating current power dynamics unless designed with explicit "power brokering" for intergenerational perspectives. This is described as "a massively underdesigned conversation."

**Specific elements:**
- Framework: Futures + Participation + Accountability; intergenerational fairness as central.
- Mechanisms: Intergenerational fairness assessments, parliamentary committees of the future, futures-based citizen engagement.
- Contradiction: Participation framed as epistemically superior, not just democratically necessary.

**Key quotations:** *"Representative democracy fails to represent a key stakeholder, which is future generations."* (IFP3, Cat Tully)

## Theme 4: Network Weaving — The Five Cs and System Stewardship

**Theme summary:** Cat describes "network weaving" as a distinct practice developed over 12 years. The five Cs: common purpose, community mapping, cultivating trust, coordination mechanisms, collaboration mechanisms. SOIF acts as "system steward" holding these elements without controlling the networks. The practice acknowledges that networks change over time and change is easier when "riding dynamics rather than in a static condition." Context matters: Finland self-organised with three natural leaders; Portugal required SOIF to fund a dedicated network weaver.

**Detailed explanation:** The system steward role is neither leader nor facilitator but a holder of infrastructure. In Finland, SITRA (government organisation), Demos (think tank), and FINGO (grassroots) naturally self-organised around three shared principles: non-politicisation, whole-of-society approach, and intergenerational social contract. In Portugal, "it's been really difficult to get anybody to kind of lean into that role because I think people really struggle with the value of network weaving." SOIF had to fund a network weaver role. In Spain, similar support was needed. The Italy case is emerging but not yet an official hub.

**Specific elements:**
- Practices: Five Cs, system stewardship, country hub activation, seed funding.
- Territories: Finland (self-organising), Portugal (required support), Spain (similar), Italy (not yet official hub).
- Actors: SITRA, Demos Helsinki, FINGO (Finland), country hub leads.

**Key quotations:** *"Network weaving tells you that you need a system steward that holds five things."* (IFP3, Cat Tully)

## Theme 6: The DX Foundation — Intentional Closure as Legacy

**Theme summary:** Cat describes the DX Foundation for Democracy and Sustainable Development as a case of intentional organisational mortality. After mainstreaming its niche agenda (the democracy-sustainable development overlap), the board chose to close and transfer €35,000 to SOIF for two fellowships/year over five years, with former board members as mentors. Cat: *"It's a beautiful intergenerational story."* She connects this to broader patterns: MacArthur's withdrawal from nuclear counter-proliferation triggered consolidation, but foresight exercises helped some organisations navigate successful mergers.

**Detailed explanation:** The DX Foundation had played a catalytic role — 2014 manifesto, influencing the Network for Institutions on Future Generations, profiling work in Hungary, Israel, Wales "way before anybody else." When the agenda mainstreamed, they chose closure rather than persistence. Cat highlights gender: "this stuff works because of the invisible emotional labor and it's often people of color and women who are prepared to do that invisible emotional labor who do it." The case illustrates "what to do when [an agenda] starts institutionalizing more broadly in society. And you want to kind of build a legacy, but no longer do the heavy lifting."

**Specific elements:**

## Theme 7: Foresight as Practice for Navigating Polarisation

**Theme summary:** Cat argues foresight uniquely navigates polarisation by creating spaces oriented toward shared futures rather than contested presents: "We may disagree fundamentally about everything but there is a common interest that we have for a future for our children." She used this in Ukraine war contexts, bringing together participants from Sudan, US, Russia, Ukraine, Israel. During COVID, SOIF explicitly countered doom narratives: "we can't colonize the future with our misery, we need to have space for reimagining positive futures." The practice helps networks remain "looser" — absorbing heterogeneity that would fracture tighter forms.

**Detailed explanation:** The mechanism is temporal distancing: futures work creates distance from present conflicts, allowing common ground in shared future concerns. This is not a claim that foresight resolves conflict, but that it creates a different kind of space — oriented to agency and hope. Cat contrasts SOIF's approach with organisations that take institutional political positions: "we avoid making comments." The non-negotiables are tolerance, empathy, psychological safety — values Cat insists must be enforced: "you need to be quite, sometimes, not compromised and not democratic about those fucking values."

**Specific elements:**
- Events: Future of Peace and Security (participants from Sudan, US, Russia, Ukraine, Israel); COVID-era positive futures spaces.
- Practices: Temporal distancing, psychological safety, tolerance and empathy as non-negotiable values.
- Contradiction: Avoiding political positions while enforcing specific values.

**Key quotations:** *"We may disagree fundamentally about everything but there is a common interest that we have for a future for our children."* (IFP3, Cat Tully)
