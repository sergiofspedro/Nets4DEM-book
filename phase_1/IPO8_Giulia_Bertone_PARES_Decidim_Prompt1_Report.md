## Theme 1: The Garage-to-Enterprise Trajectory

### Summary
Bertone's relationship with Decidim began informally—installing the platform at home with her developer husband for "garage" experiments serving very small municipalities. Over time, the need for robust, secure deployments forced a transition to professional technology partners, illustrating the maturation path from enthusiast-led adoption to enterprise-grade service provision.

### Detailed Explanation
Bertone describes a trajectory that mirrors the broader evolution of open-source participation platforms in Italy. She first encountered online participation tools through a civic network foundation using OpenDCM, but Decidim captured her attention when she "fell in love" with its capabilities at an accessible cost. Her initial approach was informal: "I started using it by installing it at home, basically, I'm lucky enough to have a developer husband who knows a little about it."

This grassroots approach worked for small municipalities with limited needs. But as demand grew—Vignola and other cities required "more robust solutions from a security standpoint"—she turned to PARES and other technological partners. This transition brought "a leap in terms of quality and also in the certainty of having expert technicians capable of overseeing the various fronts."

However, the formalisation also introduced costs: "the fact that there are companies you have to turn to in order to guarantee a certain level of technical fluidity is a problem, it's also a disadvantage." The multi-tenant solution offered by the Catalan model (AOC) represents, for her, the ideal middle ground—professional infrastructure without prohibitive individual costs.

### Specific Elements
- **Starting point**: Home installation, developer husband, garage experimentation
- **First clients**: Very small Italian municipalities
- **Transition trigger**: Need for security and robustness
- **Professional partner**: PARES enterprise
- **Cost trade-off**: Quality assurance vs. financial burden
- **Ideal model**: Multi-tenant infrastructure (Catalan AOC model)

### Key Quotations
- "I started using it by installing it at home, basically."
- "When the need arose to rely on more robust solutions from a security standpoint, like PARES, we turned to technological partners."
- "It comes at a cost because the fact that there are companies you have to turn to is a problem, it's also a disadvantage."

## Theme 2: The Community as Safety Net and Quality Guarantee

### Summary
For Bertone, the MetaDecidim community is not a peripheral benefit but the central reason for choosing Decidim over alternatives. The community ensures continuous software updates, provides a forum for feature discussions, and—most importantly—creates a sense of shared endeavour. The alternative—building a proprietary fork—would mean losing "the comparison with the various parties who are thinking about this software in a more holistic way."

### Detailed Explanation
The value of the community is existential for a small enterprise like PARES: "the fact that the software is always up-to-date—the value of the community that exists around it—is extremely important for us." In an ecosystem where proprietary platforms can become obsolete when their vendor discontinues support, Decidim's community-driven development model provides continuity.

Bertone and her team have contemplated forking: "we've often asked ourselves, 'OK, but maybe with some computer scientists we could make our own Decidim like this.'" But each time, they concluded it would mean "leaving aside the comparison with the various parties who are thinking about this software in a more holistic way."

The community also provides design direction: "it seems to me that we're really keeping pace" with design trends. The practical benefits are tangible: as an official partner since January, PARES gained access to shared promotional materials, open design files, and Italian translations—resources "I wouldn't have expected them to be open about."

### Specific Elements
- **Primary value**: Continuous updates and community-driven development
- **Fork consideration**: Rejected due to loss of holistic perspective
- **Design pace**: Keeping up with trends through community
- **Tangible benefits**: Shared files, translations, promotional materials
- **Partnership status**: Official PARES partner since January
- **Trust**: Community provides confidence in long-term viability

### Key Quotations
- "You feel like you're not alone in having identified the need for that feature."
- "Being part of a community, even from a software development perspective, is very important to us."
- "The question of whether I should stay within or move elsewhere has never arisen."

## Theme 3: Time Poverty and the Gap Between Community Potential and Participation

### Summary
A candid admission: Bertone follows MetaDecidim activities—development priority gathering, online meetings, annual festivals—but does not actively participate due to lack of time. She recognises that the barrier is not complexity ("very little was needed") but bandwidth. This illustrates a structural tension in community-based open-source projects: the members who most need the community often have the least capacity to contribute to it.

### Detailed Explanation
When asked about participation in Decidim's governance—the priority-setting process, the meta.decidim discussions—Bertone is honest: "it's my fault, our fault, that we don't have time to keep up with it." She sees development priorities being discussed and knows her voice could shape them, but cannot find the hours.

The interviewer probes whether the barrier is complexity: "did you say to yourself, 'Well, I can find the time to participate,' or did you realize it was such a complex situation that it would take a whole lot of time?" Bertone clarifies: "No, no, I realized that very little was needed and that I am physically in a condition where I cannot find the time."

This is a structural problem for communities that depend on active contribution: the small enterprises and practitioners who implement Decidim for clients are exactly those who are overstretched. They benefit from the community but cannot feed back into it at the level the community's health requires.

### Specific Elements
- **Self-assessment**: Active follower, not active participant
- **Barrier**: Bandwidth, not complexity
- **Structure**: Small enterprises are overstretched
- **Governance gap**: Cannot contribute to development priority-setting
- **MetaDecidim awareness**: Sees proposals, discussions, but cannot join
- **Community health issue**: Silent majority of practitioners

### Key Quotations
- "It's my fault, our fault, that we don't have time to keep up with it."
- "I follow, I see, but unfortunately I haven't had time to actively participate yet."
- "I realized that very little was needed and that I am physically in a condition where I cannot find the time."

## Theme 4: Decidim Fest as a Community Anchor

### Summary
The annual Decidim Fest in Barcelona is, for Bertone, the most tangible expression of the community's value. Despite initial anxiety about entering "a context of unfamiliar people who had perhaps been there for centuries," she found genuine openness—even to newcomers—and discovered that the festival serves both as a networking space and as an inspiration engine for local innovation.

### Detailed Explanation
Bertone attended the 2023 Decidim Fest with some apprehension: "I was a bit worried about entering a context of unfamiliar people who had perhaps been there for centuries." The fear of a closed, cliquish community proved unfounded. She encountered "a great openness, even to new people… a great willingness and welcome."

The festival's format—300 participants, presentations of diverse experiences, networking opportunities—served multiple functions. It was a marketplace of ideas: "there was a wide array of different experiences, and I found it incredibly enriching and inspiring." It facilitated collaboration: a CERV funding call was circulated, and members self-organised around it. It also raised ambition: the Municipality of Parma attended "to, in inverted commas, learn what can be done."

Bertone expresses a desire to "bring the format of the party forward to other times" and organise it at national or regional levels. The festival model—combining inspiration, networking, and capacity-building—is, in her view, transferable.

### Specific Elements
- **Initial anxiety**: Fear of closed community
- **Experience**: Openness and welcome
- **Scale**: 300 participants
- **Functions**: Inspiration, networking, partnership-building
- **CERV call**: Example of organic collaboration
- **Transferability**: Desires national/regional versions

### Key Quotations
- "There was a great openness, even to new people—a great willingness and welcome."
- "I found it incredibly enriching and inspiring to have them, to bring them there."
- "It would be wonderful to bring the format of the party forward to other times."

## Theme 5: The Multi-Tenant Frustration – Unlocking Regional Potential

### Summary
Bertone's deepest frustration is the Emilia-Romagna region's failure to implement multi-tenant Decidim, despite having the infrastructure and the need. The region uses Decidim primarily as a transparency tool for funded projects rather than as a participatory platform. The multi-tenant model—already proven in Catalonia—would allow the region's 300+ municipalities to run their own participatory processes on shared infrastructure, but political will has not materialised.

### Detailed Explanation
Emilia-Romagna is "an advanced region, with a lot of interesting experiences," yet its Decidim deployment is "still at the level of a major transparency operation for the processes it funds." The platform requires funded projects to upload minimum content, but the participatory potential remains untapped.

Bertone has pushed for multi-tenant repeatedly: "I've spoken with D'Alena, with De Rio, but who knows, we're still there, waiting." A municipality recently called her to ask "When is it coming?" because they "can't use the space currently guaranteed by the regional platform" and are considering a private solution.

The interviewer frames this as a structural failure: without multi-tenant, "you decentralize, but in a way that isn't subsidiary—not conceived according to an order of subsidiarity in which you help multiply the effects." The region could collect all participation data in a standardised format, enabling comparison, mutual learning, and evidence-based policymaking. Instead, each municipality is left to improvise.

The contrast with Catalonia is sharp: "Catalonia has the AOC which has a real interest in growing participatory culture alongside technological culture." Emilia-Romagna has the technology but lacks the vision to deploy it strategically.

### Specific Elements
- **Current use**: Transparency portal for funded projects
- **Untapped potential**: Real participatory processes at municipal scale
- **Multi-tenant benefit**: Shared infrastructure, standardised data, autonomy + centralisation
- **Push effort**: Repeated proposals from Bertone, no political response
- **Consequence**: Municipalities seeking private solutions
- **Catalonia comparison**: AOC provides integrated model
- **Interviewer's role**: Commissioned to write about intermediate scales

### Key Quotations
- "The current use of the platform is still at the level of a major transparency operation."
- "I've pushed for it several times, and it's right."
- "There's a lack of awareness of what could be done with the platform."
- "The latest news was, 'We'll get there at the end of the mandate, don't worry.'"

## Theme 6: Parma's Neighbourhood Workshops – When it Works

### Summary
In contrast to the regional frustration, Bertone offers a concrete success story: the Municipality of Parma's transformation of its neighbourhood workshops through Decidim. The platform brought order to a chaotic system of ad hoc communication between citizens and councillors, creating a structured, transparent, and institutionally accountable process for neighbourhood-level participation.

### Detailed Explanation
Parma used Decidim to reform its neighbourhood workshops—the former CCCs (voluntary citizen committees) dating from a 1956 framework. The workshops were redesigned as "neighbourhood workshops that actually have the decision makers in the house." The platform became the backbone of this transformation.

The mechanism works because it is hybrid: "The proposals are actually uploaded and then discussed in person in the workshops." The Participation Office oversees the proposals, evaluates them, and "provides a public response." This created accountability where none existed before: "a mechanism has been triggered for these workshops to function, one that communicates closely with the platform."

Bertone did not expect the model to work as well as it did: "I never expected it to work this way." The result was that the platform "contributed not only to transparency, but also to ordering communication flows." Instead of workshop members calling councillors directly—creating informal influence channels—the platform now serves as the institutional record: "comments are published, outcomes are published, there is a public space."

This case illustrates that Decidim's value is not in replacing in-person participation but in structuring it—providing the scaffolding that makes informal processes legible and accountable.

### Specific Elements
- **Municipality**: Parma, Italy
- **Context**: Reform of neighbourhood workshops (ex-CCCs)
- **Hybrid model**: Proposals uploaded, discussed in person
- **Oversight**: Participation Office evaluates and responds publicly
- **Outcome**: Ordered communication flows, reduced informal influence
- **Surprise factor**: Exceeded expectations

### Key Quotations
- "I never expected it to work this way, but it does."
- "It has contributed not only to transparency, but also to ordering communication flows at the level of the participatory process."
- "There is a public space that allows the administration to take institutional ownership of this proposal."

## Theme 7: The National Level – Legitimacy Without Substance

### Summary
Bertone's experience with Decidim at the Italian national level (through Formez and the Department of Civil Service) was deeply disappointing. The platform was used as a glorified questionnaire tool, with celebration of minimal metrics (50 completed questionnaires). However, the national adoption did serve one critical function: it legitimised Decidim as reliable technology in the Italian public sector ecosystem.

### Detailed Explanation
The contract with Formez promised to "bring together a few experiences, communities, and technology use," but "it fizzled out." Bertone describes herself as "very disappointed, so to speak. Both in terms of how the platform is used, its outcomes, and its impact."

The depth of the disappointment is captured in her reaction to the metrics: "there was a lot of celebration when 50 questionnaires were filled out nationwide, but what are we talking about?" The platform's participatory potential was reduced to the lowest-common-denominator function.

Yet the national adoption was not without value. It served a legitimising role: "the fact that a central administration, even within the limits of its use, has focused on the platform and adopted it for certain processes seems to have legitimized and emphasized the importance of tools like these, also in terms of their reliability." This legitimacy helped raise awareness and made it easier for local administrations to justify Decidim adoption.

The paradox is that national-level adoption created an enabling condition for local innovation without itself innovating. Bertone sees the real potential at the local level, where "there are some small pilot projects you can carve out for yourself where you find a particularly free, flexible, dynamic environment."

### Specific Elements
- **Contract**: Formez / Department of Civil Service
- **Early promise**: Community-building, multi-experience platform
- **Reality**: Questionnaire tool
- **Metrics**: Celebration of 50 responses
- **Positive outcome**: Legitimisation and awareness-raising
- **Real innovation locus**: Local, not national

### Key Quotations
- "I'm very disappointed. Both in terms of how the platform is used, its outcomes, and its impact."
- "There was a lot of celebration when 50 questionnaires were filled out nationwide, but what are we talking about?"
- "The technology is the least of the problems… it comes after having developed a method."

## Theme 8: The Participatory Culture Deficit

### Summary
Bertone argues that the primary barrier to Decidim's effective use in Italy is not technological but cultural. Administrations do not understand participation as anything more than "a questionnaire about a policy you're developing." The platform becomes a site rather than a space for genuine democratic engagement. Training and awareness-raising are part of PARES's service package, but the deeper problem is political will.

### Detailed Explanation
The interviewer asks why Decidim is used differently in Catalonia than in Italy, and Bertone locates the difference in participatory culture: "the very fact that if you imagine and understand participation only as a questionnaire about a policy you're developing, you don't even feel the need to look for tools."

Administrations approach Decidim with a magic-bullet expectation: "they often ask us, 'Do we want the platform, do we want to set it up?' It's a bit like the idea that the tool alone—boom!—will create who knows what." When the platform fails to generate participation automatically, the issue is framed as a tool problem rather than a process or political problem.

PARES includes training in its service packages—"training and awareness-raising at various levels, both for those who must use it technically, but also at the more political level." The training covers international examples, especially Spanish ones. But Bertone admits this has limited impact: "even some contexts are so backward."

The interviewer suggests a deeper problem: training should come from peer politicians, not consultants. "The only thing that breaks through the political barrier is talking to people who have the same responsibilities as you." A consultant offering training is perceived as selling a product; a peer sharing experience is perceived as offering solidarity. Bertone acknowledges this insight but has not found the organisational capacity to implement peer-to-peer models.

### Specific Elements
- **Cultural diagnosis**: Participation = questionnaire; platform = site
- **Magic-bullet expectation**: Tool alone will create participation
- **Training offered**: Technical + political, with international examples
- **Effectiveness limit**: Political culture resistant to consultant-led training
- **Peer-to-peer gap**: Politicians need to hear from other politicians
- **Catalonia difference**: Deeper participatory culture

### Key Quotations
- "If you imagine and understand participation only as a questionnaire, then you don't even feel the need to look for tools."
- "There's often the idea that the tool alone—boom!—will create who knows what."
- "The only thing that breaks through the political barrier is talking to people who have the same responsibilities as you."

## Theme 9: The Provider-Community Tension

### Summary
Bertone's dual role—as a service provider selling Decidim implementations and as a community member—creates a structural tension. She can see opportunities to use the community as a catalyst for raising awareness and pushing administrations toward more ambitious use, but her capacity is consumed by service delivery. The community's potential as a collective advocacy force remains largely untapped.

### Detailed Explanation
The interviewer asks whether community members can function as "provocateurs or catalysts for cultural growth" beyond their role as service providers. Bertone's answer reveals the tension: she includes training in her packages and shares international examples, but the model is fundamentally transactional. The community could be used more strategically—for example, organising peer-to-peer exchanges among Italian municipalities using Decidim—but neither the time nor the institutional framework exists.

She is planning a meeting "for those who use Decidim to prepare participatory budgets, to share ideas, push, and demonstrate what can be done." But this remains locally focused: "we're still at a local level, not central administration."

The interviewer pushes further: "being part of a community, even just as a provider, can somehow help you convince others who then convince the legislator." The community could be a multiplier, amplifying the voice of practitioners who individually lack the leverage to shift institutional behaviour. Bertone agrees in principle but cannot find the bandwidth to operationalise it.

### Specific Elements
- **Dual role**: Service provider and community member
- **Current approach**: Training with international examples
- **Untapped potential**: Collective advocacy, peer-to-peer exchange
- **Barrier**: Time, capacity, institutional framework
- **Planned intervention**: Meeting of PB practitioners using Decidim
- **Scale limitation**: Local focus, not national or community-wide

### Key Quotations
- "We're planning a meeting for those who use Decidim to prepare participatory budgets, to share ideas, push, and demonstrate what can be done."
- "I'm not someone who works at a national level, so I don't know if I'm exaggerating."

## Theme 10: Digital Identity Barriers and the Exclusion of Young People

### Summary
Bertone identifies a specific technical barrier to participation: the requirement for SPID (digital identity system) or CIE (electronic identity card) login to use advanced Decidim features. This effectively excludes young people, who do not yet possess these credentials. The only anonymous participation tool is the questionnaire, which reinforces the trivialisation of participation.

### Detailed Explanation
The integration of Decidim with Italy's national digital identity systems (SPID and CIE) was intended to ensure security and accountability. In practice, it creates an age barrier: "speed is a problem for minors because they can't use it with the CIE for school services." Young people, who should be a primary audience for school-based participatory budgeting and other youth engagement initiatives, are locked out of meaningful participation.

Bertone notes that "there could be alternative login options, but it's too complicated, it's not done." The consequence is that "the only tool in Decidim that suggests you don't log in and contribute anonymously is always a nice questionnaire." This reinforces the pattern where the platform's most sophisticated functions go unused and the simplest—and least transformative—function becomes the default.

The frustration is not unique to Decidim or Italy but reflects a broader tension between security requirements and democratic inclusion. When identity verification becomes a prerequisite for participation, the most marginalised and the youngest are systematically excluded.

### Specific Elements
- **Barrier**: SPID/CIE login requirement
- **Excluded group**: Young people (minors without digital ID)
- **Available workaround**: Anonymous questionnaires only
- **Consequence**: Trivialisation of participation
- **Structural issue**: Security vs. inclusion
- **Not unique to Decidim**: Systemic challenge in digital participation

### Key Quotations
- "This thing cuts out a segment of people, especially young people, for whom there could be alternative login options, but it's too complicated, it's not done."
- "The only tool in Decidim that suggests you don't log in and contribute anonymously is always a nice questionnaire."
