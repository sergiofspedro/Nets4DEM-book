## Theme 1: OGP as a Multi-Actor Partnership — Governments and Civil Society Co-Governance

**Theme summary:** Tim describes OGP's distinctive model: a partnership between governments and civil society that is not a traditional membership network but a co-governance arrangement where both categories of actors have formal roles. OGP operates at the intersection of state and civil society, requiring participating governments to co-create action plans with civil society organisations. This model embeds networked governance principles into formal state processes.

**Detailed explanation:** OGP was founded in 2011 and now includes over 75 national governments and hundreds of local governments, plus thousands of civil society organisations. The co-creation requirement — that action plans must be developed jointly by government and civil society — is the mechanism through which OGP attempts to institutionalise networked governance within state structures. Tim's role bridges the democratic participation agenda with the open government framework, connecting citizen participation, deliberative processes, and transparency.

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: OGP, national governments (75+), local governments, civil society organisations.
- Practices: Co-creation of action plans; multi-stakeholder governance.
- Problems: Maintaining civil society independence while embedded in government processes.

**Key quotations:** Context places OGP at the intersection of the open government and democratic participation movements.

## Theme 2: Networks of Networks — How OGP Connects to OIDP, People Powered, and EPD

**Theme summary:** Tim's interview explores how OGP relates to other transnational networks working on democracy and participation — OIDP (local governments), People Powered (civil society practitioners), and EPD (European democracy support). The interviewer frames these as overlapping but distinct spaces: *"sometimes our networks, sometimes our networks of networks and sometimes our network of people and organisation, sometime only of cities."* Tim's position at OGP gives him a unique vantage point on how these different network geometries interact, compete, and complement each other.

**Detailed explanation:** The interviewer explicitly situates Tim's interview within the Nets4DEM project's interest in network typologies: "they have in common that they are transnational... not mono actors... they put together different typologies of actors even if they have a priority maybe on cities or on professionals... they accept the dialogue between different categories of actors." OGP's distinctive contribution to this ecosystem is its formal institutional link to governments — unlike People Powered (civil society-focused) or OIDP (city government-focused), OGP binds governments to civil society through formal co-creation commitments. This creates both leverage (governments are accountable for commitments) and limitation (civil society organisations must operate within government-defined processes).

**Specific elements:**
- Networks: OGP, OIDP, People Powered, EPD.
- Practices: Overlapping network participation; multi-network engagement strategies.
- Contradiction: Networks compete for attention and resources while collaborating on shared agendas.

**Key quotations:** The interviewer frames the question of how networks "are moving in the world of this network which are at the same time fragmented but convergent and often overlapping." (Interviewer, IFP16 context)
