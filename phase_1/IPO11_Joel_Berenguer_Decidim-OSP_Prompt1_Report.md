## Theme 1: The Dual Nature of Decidim — Technological Product and Community Network

**Theme summary:** Joel's interview explores Decidim's dual identity: it is simultaneously a technological product (open-source software) and a community/network (MetaDecidim). The interviewer frames this as distinctive: "in the case of the Decidim the main issue is that there are two joint goals, which are one is finding a way to maintain a technological product constantly active... and also ethical... and then there is another that is making it be used." This dual nature creates specific challenges — the community must keep the software updated and ethically aligned, while also ensuring adoption by public administrations and citizens.

**Detailed explanation:** Open Source Politics (OSP) is a French enterprise that implements Decidim for clients — part of the ecosystem of Decidim service providers. Joel's position as "point of contact" in the Nets4Dem project connects him to the broader research agenda. The interviewer explicitly contrasts Decidim with other network forms in the corpus: networks that "come from previous project and then transform into network connection, which have different shape of platforms, hubs." Decidim's product-centric origin (software developed for Barcelona's participatory budgeting, then spun into an independent open-source project with a community) creates a governance model where developers and technical decisions have disproportionate influence relative to end-users and governments.

**Specific elements:**
- Actors: Open Source Politics (France), Decidim/MetaDecidim, Barcelona City Council.
- Practices: Open-source software maintenance, community governance, ethical technology alignment.
- Problems: Balancing product development with community governance; ensuring adoption beyond developer community.
