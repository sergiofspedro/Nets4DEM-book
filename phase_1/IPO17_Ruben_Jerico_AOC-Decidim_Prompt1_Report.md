## Theme 2: Identity and Participation — The Tension Between Anonymous and Identified Models

### Summary
The most substantive tension Rubén discusses is the question of whether participants in digital participatory processes should be anonymous or identified. AOC has chosen to implement an identity verification system for Decidim, requiring participants to authenticate — a deliberate departure from Decidim's traditional permissive model of anonymous participation. This reflects a deeper debate about the quality, legitimacy, and responsibility of participation.

### Detailed Explanation
Rubén describes AOC's decision to develop an identity module for Decidim, which requires participants to identify themselves through the Catalan public identity system (similar to FranceConnect or Italy's SPID). The rationale is multiple: protection of the legitimacy of participatory outcomes, prevention of abuse (as seen in social media), alignment with traditional face-to-face participation where participants are known, and compliance with digital rights and public service obligations. Giovanni challenges this, raising examples from Brazil where police officers could not propose changes to security laws for fear of retaliation if identified. Rubén acknowledges the trade-off but maintains that identification is not fundamentally opposed to protection of minority views — "if I go to a participatory physical process, I think could be part of the moment to see my face, to hear my voice." The tension remains unresolved between the Decidim community's tradition of open, anonymous participation and AOC's public-sector requirement for accountable, identified engagement.

### Specific Elements

- **Rationale**: Legitimacy, accountability, prevention of abuse, alignment with public service norms
- **Critique**: May discourage participation from vulnerable groups (e.g., police officers, whistleblowers)
- **Giovanni's counterpoint**: Technical solutions can separate identification from public anonymity (known to the system, anonymous to other participants)

## Theme 3: The Multi-Tenant Model — Decidim as a Shared Infrastructure for Many Administrations

### Summary
AOC operates a multi-tenant Decidim model, providing the platform as a shared service to multiple administrations simultaneously. This creates economies of scale and lowers the barrier for small municipalities to offer digital participation, but also raises questions about the balance between standardisation and local adaptation, and the relationship between AOC and the broader Decidim community.

### Detailed Explanation
The multi-tenant model means that AOC hosts and manages Decidim instances for 45+ different public entities from a central infrastructure. This is distinct from each municipality running its own instance. The advantage is that small municipalities (especially those under 2,000 inhabitants, served by the Diputació de Barcelona) can offer digital participation without technical capacity. The challenge is that AOC's priorities for development (identity module, public statistics) may not align perfectly with the priorities of the Decidim community or other providers. Rubén describes how AOC acts somewhat autonomously from the Generalitat de Catalunya and the Decidim Association — "we form part of the Generalitat de Catalunya in terms of public services to local governments" but also maintains separate relationships with provincial governments and the community.

### Specific Elements
- **Shared infrastructure**: Central hosting, local front-ends
- **Small municipality inclusion**: Municipalities under 2,000 inhabitants get access through the Diputació de Barcelona
- **Autonomous development**: AOC develops its own modules (identity, statistics) outside the core Decidim roadmap
- **Comparison with other providers**: AOC completes the coverage that other providers (Diputació de Barcelona, private companies) do not reach
- **Open data register**: Publicly tracks all instances as a transparency measure

## Theme 4: Decidim's Untapped Potential — The Gap Between Features and Actual Use

### Summary
Rubén agrees with Giovanni's observation that Decidim's potential is far from fully realised in practice. Most municipalities use only basic functions (voting, proposals) while advanced features (co-writing documents, deliberative tools) remain underutilised. His analysis of why this happens goes beyond the usual explanations of training deficit or lack of political will — he points to the mismatch between the technology's design and citizens' actual interests and habits.

### Detailed Explanation
When asked about the gap between Decidim's potential and its actual use, Rubén offers a nuanced perspective. He argues that the underutilisation is not simply a training problem but reflects a deeper disconnect between how the platform is designed and how people actually engage with digital tools in their daily lives. He compares Decidim's user experience unfavourably with consumer apps like Spotify — "how to use it through other solutions like Spotify" — suggesting that participatory platforms must compete with the seamless experience of commercial applications. Crucially, he argues that participation follows interest: "if you ask someone about something that is not interested in, the result could be not participate." The key, in his view, is to reduce the distance between citizens and government, making participation relevant to people's actual concerns, and to design for mobile-first, time-efficient interactions. He does not see a simple technical fix but a need for a deeper sociological and design rethinking.

### Specific Elements
- **Feature gap**: Advanced Decidim functions (co-writing, deliberation) rarely used
- **Rubén's diagnosis**: Not just training — design mismatch with user habits
- **Competing with consumer apps**: Decidim must match the user experience of Spotify, etc.
- **Interest-driven participation**: People participate when the topic matters to them
- **Mobile-first**: Need for app-based, time-efficient engagement
- **Proximity**: "Short distance from population to the administrators"
- **AOC's response**: Training administrators, public statistics, identity module — but recognises these are partial solutions
