## Theme 1: People Powered as a Community of Practice with Personal Connection

**Theme summary:** Diana describes People Powered as a network that prioritises personal relationships and individual wellbeing. She highlights the practice of starting meetings with icebreakers focused on how people are feeling — "the icebreaker, are always centred on the person, their feeling, the status in front of the changes around them." This person-centred culture creates a distinctive emotional bond: some members describe it as having "a sort of umbilical relation to People Powered... like if it was a person of the family." This emphasis on human connection differentiates People Powered from more institutional or technocratic networks.

**Detailed explanation:** The interview with Diana (conducted jointly with the interviewer Giovanni) explores the cultural dimension of People Powered's networking model. The interviewer notes that this person-centred approach is strategically significant: "in a bad moment for the world to search for similar people with similar feelings, with similar fears... fears and feelings can be a glue inside a network, where the other connectors can be also weak." The network's care for individuals — checking in on their emotional state, creating spaces for personal sharing — functions as an alternative binding mechanism when other connectors (funding, political alignment, institutional mandates) are fragile.

**Specific elements:**
- Practices: Icebreakers focused on personal feelings; person-centred meeting culture.
- Problems: Maintaining human connection at scale; balancing personal care with professional efficiency.

**Key quotations:** *"People Powered take a lot care of the individuals. They always ask in the beginning of the meeting, how are you feeling."* (IPO7 context, interviewer)
