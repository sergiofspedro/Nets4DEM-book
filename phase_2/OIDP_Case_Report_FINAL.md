## 1. Case Overview

**What OIDP is:** The International Observatory of Participatory Democracy is a global network of cities, local governments, universities, and civil society organizations dedicated to promoting, exchanging, and improving local participatory democracy. It operates an annual rotating conference and a Best Practice Award (20 editions), structurally integrated under UCLG (United Cities and Local Governments).

**Central sources:** Four informants held formal OIDP roles:
- **Adrià Duarte Griño (ITR1, Coordinator since 2013):** Current Coordinator since ~2013. Contemporary perspective on governance, the award as observation tool, UCLG integration, and the defensive turn.
- **Fernando Pindado (IFP6, Secretary General 2015-2019):** Secretary General 2015–2019. Deepest historical view, critical on name and individual-institution paradox, architect of UCLG integration strategy.
- **Melissa Pomeroy (ITR3, Staff 2004-2007):** Technical staff 2004–2007. Sole source for Urbal A/B project period, award creation, initial UCLG institutionalization, Nanterre exclusion incident.
- **Marc Serra (IFP11b, Secretary General):** Secretary General during Barcelona term. Legal-administrative perspective on procurement rules and UCLG as legal shelter.

**Supplementary:** IPO1 (Andres Falck) compares OIDP vs People Powered. IPO4 (Carla Bezerra) compares OIDP/Decidim/PP political positioning. IFP5/ITR4 trace People Powered origins to an OIDP conference. IPO7, IPO6, IFP10, IFP20 contain minimal OIDP content.

**What the case is mainly about:** OIDP embodies the paradox of a network born as EU project with a specific name ('Observatory') that outlasted its original structure and now operates through substitute mechanisms (award as observation, conferences as community). It has survived 25 years through adaptive strategies: institutional nesting within UCLG, lean resourcing, managed openness to diverse political regimes, and implicit reliance on committed individuals over formal institutional membership.

## 2. Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

### Dimension 1: Organisational Identity, Trajectory, and Positionality
**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño; IFP6, Fernando Pindado; ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy; IFP11b, Marc Serra; IPO4, Carla Bezerra; IPO1, Andres Falck)

*Origins and name:* OIDP was created in 2001 as EU-funded project (Urbal A, then Urbal B) led by Barcelona, aiming to establish local observatories in partner cities across Europe and Latin America (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy). When EU funding ended (~2007), Barcelona maintained the network but the local observatories model was abandoned — the name persisted through institutional inertia (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño; ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy).

Fernando Pindado states bluntly: *"There are two things I don't like about the OIDP: its name and its surname. I don't even like the name 'observatory,' because it isn't one"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). He critiques "participatory": *"democracy can only be participatory; otherwise, it wouldn't be democracy"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Adrià doesn't know why the name was kept (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Melissa notes it was never discussed (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy). The interviewer observes that "observatories are traditionally very Latin structures" (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Despite these critiques, the name has persisted 25 years.

*Three phases:* (1) **EU Project Era (2001–2007):** Funded by Urbal A/B. Nine local observatories. Small, project-driven (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño; ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy; IFP6, Fernando Pindado). (2) **Consolidation/Internationalization (2007–2015):** UCLG institutionalization from 2007 as purely bureaucratic arrangement — staff hired by UCLG, headquarters moved, no programmatic connection initially (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy). Award created (2006). OIDP Africa established (~2011) (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). (3) **Defensive/Adaptive (2015–present):** More political. UCLG Committee integration. Rotating presidency. Digital adaptation post-COVID (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño; IFP6, Fernando Pindado; IFP11b, Marc Serra).

*Self-description:* Adrià: *"We probably mentioned more the advocacy part, but we keep being of course a community of practice. And probably the people who participate, they value more the part of community of practice"* (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño).

*Comparative political positioning:* Carla Bezerra characterizes Decidim as having "a very defined political view" from the 15M movement, while People Powered has "a very broad, open political view with minimum definition." OIDP falls between — politically committed (declarations) but institutionally neutral through UCLG and its identity as a network of governments with diverse orientations (IPO4, Carla Bezerra). Andres Falck: OIDP "has always wanted to be institutional... it has become a little more institutional... more rule-based" compared to informal networks like Iberian PB meetings (IPO1, Andres Falck).

### Dimension 2: Membership, Composition, and Internal Inclusion
**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño; IFP6, Fernando Pindado; ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy; IFP11b, Marc Serra; IPO1, Andres Falck)

*Membership:* Formal membership is exclusively institutional — cities, governments, universities, NGOs. Individuals cannot register (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Associate members (universities, NGOs) existed from the beginning but "couldn't be presidents, couldn't have the technical secretariat. The leading role was always with the cities" (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy). Marc Serra confirms: "All the networks... are networks where the main players are always the cities" (IFP11b, Marc Serra).

*The individual-institution paradox:* Every core informant emphasizes individuals as the true lifeblood. Adrià: *"This network has keep working because there's persons around... The important it's the people"* (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Fernando: *"The structures that are networks of legal entities... often have more presence of the person acting on behalf of them than of the institution they claim to represent"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). He advocates: *"What I believe could be beneficial is that... the process incorporates individuals, groups, and entities — things other than cities"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Andres Falck describes individuals as "pollinating bees" carrying network relationships across institutional affiliations (IPO1, Andres Falck). Fernando's illustration: *"That's what Giovanni Allegretti does, not the Pepito Institute; it's you, as an individual, who does it"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

*Political turnover and the waste of experience:* Fernando identifies the challenge: elected representatives change, and "unfortunately, not all political parties... are committed to it" (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Marc Serra illustrates: Barcelona's reduced prioritization of participation has made it "difficult to convene this steering committee" (IFP11b, Marc Serra). Fernando proposes an alumni space: *"OIDP should facilitate a space where those who are no longer mayors can remain involved if they wish... a sort of group of retirees... who possess knowledge that could be utilized"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

*The Nanterre incident:* After leaving OIDP in 2007, Melissa attended the Nanterre conference but was denied entry: *"The Nanterre city hall wouldn't let me in because I hadn't registered, because I didn't represent any city... I found it strange, this rigidity"* (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy). This epitomizes "the waste of experience" (IFP6, Fernando Pindado) — the network erasing individual contributions once institutional affiliation ends.

*Geographic equity:* Conferences rotate regions: "The first rule is always to change region each year" (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Granollers 2022 had a specific fund for 6–7 participants from low-income countries (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Virtual participation has improved accessibility (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

*Dormant membership:* "Ulaanbaatar... participated in an award in 2004 and hasn't been seen since" but remains listed (IFP6, Fernando Pindado) — reflecting membership as loose community rather than managed list, with strategic value for perceived reach.

### Dimension 3: Governance, Coordination, and Democratic Coherence
**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño; IFP6, Fernando Pindado; ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy; IFP11b, Marc Serra)

*Secretariat and presidency:* Technical secretariat in Barcelona (city-funded); presidency rotated among conference hosts, who also paid for the conference (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy). Fernando was critical: *"I also held the position of Secretary General... frankly, it wasn't democratic. It meant that the City Council, the city that contributes the most resources, was the one... in charge of the General Secretariat"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). This led to 2018 reforms creating rotation and diversifying contributions.

*Dual-coordinator model:* In 2024–2025, Adrià became coordinator of both OIDP and UCLG Committee: *"To be honest, I have of course less time for the observatory"* (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Fernando was architect: *"We started working on how to more strongly link the OIDP to UCLG... so that OIDP would become the specialized arm... on democratic quality"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

*Decision-making and the Tehran case:* Distributed rather than centralized. When Tehran submitted an award nomination, "some juror... has an ethical question... so they decide not to evaluate that" (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). The interviewer: *"You solve through the pluralism... you let the larger community decide"* (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). This contrasts with Decidim losing partners over Palestine and People Powered refusing to take positions (IPO4, Carla Bezerra).

*UCLG as legal shelter:* Marc Serra: linking OIDP to UCLG "gave more legal credibility to the OIDP agreement file... because it gave it the legitimacy of the UCLG members" (IFP11b, Marc Serra), helping Barcelona navigate procurement scrutiny.

*Limited coordinator mandate:* Melissa: *"The person who manages the network on a daily basis... is a person without a mandate... regardless of whether they have wonderful ideas"* (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy) — a structural gap between operational capacity and strategic authority.

### Dimension 4: Resources, Formalisation, and Organisational Sustainability
**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño; IFP6, Fernando Pindado; ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy; IPO4, Carla Bezerra; IFP11b, Marc Serra)

*Funding and lean resilience:* Small budget, primarily Barcelona with EU via UCLG (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño; ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy). Adrià: *"Also the good thing is that it's so small amount of money that it's probably more easy to survive"* (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Fernando confirms the budget "wasn't an excessive burden" (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Secretariat: "It's a salary, and it covers travel" (IFP6, Fernando Pindado); "one person... plus one internal staff member" (IFP11b context).

*EU limitations:* Under UCLG framework: "We cannot participate through them to any bid... cannot sign letters in support of a project" (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño).

*Membership fees rejected:* "We made some research... we decided it was really not the good way to go" (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Fernando: the initial rejection was to avoid Barcelona's financial dominance translating into governance dominance (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

*People Powered comparison:* Carla notes PP "has proved to be extremely resilient" through diversified American fundraising and "incorporating Europe to apply to European grants" (IPO4, Carla Bezerra). Contrast: PP resilience through diversification; OIDP resilience through institutional nesting and low cost.

*Current risk:* Marc Serra flags Barcelona's deprioritization of participation has made it "difficult to convene this steering committee" (IFP11b, Marc Serra) — host-city political will remains primary vulnerability.

### Dimension 5: External Relations and Legitimacy
**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (ITR1, IFP6, IPO1, IPO4, IFP5, ITR3, IFP11b)

*Real partnerships:* (1) **People Powered:** Always present at OIDP conferences/awards (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Clara+Nikhil trace PP's origin to an OIDP Barcelona conference where a Hewlett Foundation workshop catalyzed the initiative (IFP5, Clara Bois). Andres participated in that conference (IPO1, Andres Falck). (2) **Global Democracy Coalition** via International IDEA (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). (3) **SALAR (Sweden):** Model partner — "the more clear one that we would love to replicate" with dedicated democracy section (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). (4) **OIDP Africa:** Almost an equal — "more a larger budget... more resources and they do a lot of activities" (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño).

*UCLG value:* Fernando calls it "a reputational asset, an unwritten asset that cannot be lost" (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Adrià: "Being linked to UCLG... to have this voice on the table at the UN, in many UN agendas" (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Marc Serra: legal/procurement credibility (IFP11b, Marc Serra).

*Historical silos:* Melissa reveals early OIDP isolated from other Barcelona-hosted networks (Educating Cities, Metrópolis): "There was never any substantial contact... Barcelona City Council itself... didn't have a perspective of... let's get them to talk to each other" (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy).

*Award as recruitment:* "The ones from Philippines, from Nepal — it's gonna be impossible to get them from another world" (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño).

*Conference as marketplace:* Fernando: "It's good that it brings together people whose economic activity is related to this with those who actually influence or make political decisions. I think that's a virtuous relationship" (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

### Dimension 6: Knowledge, Learning, and Mediation
**Presence: PARTIALLY PRESENT** (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño; ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy; IFP6, Fernando Pindado)

*Award as de facto observatory:* The most analytically significant finding. Interviewer: *"In the absence of specific funding to do monitoring work as an observatory, the Best Practice Award is in reality still the most important part of your observation deck. You are an observatory thanks to the Best Practice Award."* Adrià: *"Absolutely"* (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). The award generates data others methods failed to produce: *"Sometimes we did a survey... and it's really not working at all. And the award is really working on that sense"* (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño).

*Longitudinal tracking:* *"Three years ago they were submitting this practice, now it's evolved to another one"* (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño).

*Quality standards:* Early debate on quality criteria: "common quality criteria for these different experiences... some minimum standards" (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy) — establishing rigor without imposing single normative standard.

*Self-historicization:* 25th anniversary infographic (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño); Fernando's written contribution (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Fernando: "the documentation OIDP produces is very interesting and useful for municipalities" (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

*Digital limits:* COVID forced online events, but post-pandemic digital activity decreased (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Carla's observation about Decidim's European-centric design — "excess of information" not suited for mobile-first Global South (IPO4, Carla Bezerra) — has implications for OIDP's knowledge function.

### Dimension 7: Adaptation, Resilience, and Transformative Capacity
**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (ITR1, IFP6, ITR3, IFP11b, IPO4)

*Structural transformation:* From EU project to independent network (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy), observatories model to award/conference model (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño), Barcelona-centric to UCLG-integrated with rotating presidency (IFP6, Fernando Pindado), single to dual coordinator (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño).

*Defensive turn:* Adrià describes shift from optimistic to defensive framing: "how we can use participation to face rising authoritarian threats" (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Fernando contextualizes within V-DEM data on democratic decline (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Carla notes PP's parallel challenge (IPO4, Carla Bezerra). However, core activities haven't fundamentally changed — the defensive turn may be more narrative adaptation than substantive reorientation.

*UCLG merger as adaptation:* Adrià frames as necessity+opportunity (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Fernando as culmination of his strategy (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Marc Serra: *"It's a good thing that UCLG existed to support the OEDP, because with that fragmentation you can't govern an international entity"* (IFP11b, Marc Serra).

*Lean resilience empirical basis:* OIDP survived Barcelona's multiple political transitions (Comú → PSOE → current) because its marginal cost escaped political scrutiny.

*Merger caution:* Fernando: *"I would advocate more for joint programs... in a marriage of convenience that could end badly. Marriages rarely end well"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado) vs. Adrià's experimental dual-coordinator model.

### Dimension 8: Commoning
**Presence: MARGINALLY PRESENT** (ITR1, IFP6, IPO4)

*Implicit commoning:* Open membership, free award, rotating conferences, knowledge sharing without paywalls embody commoning principles. Adrià: "we're open to all the cities in the world" (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño).

*Commons of democratic practice:* OIDP's "common quality criteria" (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy) represent an attempt to define shared standards for a commons of democratic practice.

*Knowledge as shared resource:* Award pools innovations globally. Fernando: documentation useful beyond formal membership (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

*Absent:* Explicit commoning theory, reciprocity mechanisms, shared ownership. Decidim's open-source philosophy — developments "given back to the community" (IPO4, Carla Bezerra) — represents a related but distinct commoning regime.

### Dimension 9: Impacts
**Presence: MARGINALLY PRESENT** (ITR1, IFP6, IPO1, ITR3)

*Visibility:* Award (20 editions), conferences (25 years). Andres: "everybody knows about... IOPD" (IPO1 context).

*Cognitive impact:* 2018 Local Democracy Agenda as "theoretical reference" (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Early quality standards generated "interesting and very political discussion" (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy).

*Alliance strengthening:* Dual-coordinator role increased UCLG Committee-linked award nominations: "Vienna, many other practices" (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño).

*Unexpected:* Award as recruitment tool (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño); conference as marketplace for facilitation services (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

*Gaps:* No specific outcome metrics, policy attributions, or impact evaluations. Requires document analysis or member-city interviews.

## 3. Cross-Cutting Analytical Themes

### Theme 1: The Name That Outlived Its Meaning — And Found a New One
**Where:** Dimensions 1, 6. **Sources:** ITR1, IFP6, ITR3.

The 'Observatory' name persists because the award functions as de facto observation. Fernando: *"OIDP isn't a place for analyzing reality; it's more of a space, a network for exchanging experiences"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Organizational naming decoupled from function but re-grounded through substitute practice.

### Theme 2: The Individual-Institution Paradox
**Where:** Dimensions 2, 3, 8. **Sources:** ITR1, IFP6, ITR3, IPO1.

OIDP is formally institution-of-institutions yet operates as network-of-individuals. Fernando's definitive formulation: *"That's what Giovanni Allegretti does, not the Pepito Institute; it's you, as an individual"* (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Manifests in: (a) institutional membership vs. individual participation (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño; IPO1, Andres Falck); (b) Nanterre exclusion — network erases former staff (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy); (c) mortality — individuals die, institutions lose knowledge (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Fernando proposes alumni space (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

### Theme 3: Lean Resilience — The Strategic Advantage of Being Small
**Where:** Dimensions 4, 7. **Sources:** ITR1, IFP6, IFP11b.

*"We're small, so we can easily survive"* (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). OIDP's minimal budget enables survival through Barcelona's political transitions. Marc Serra: UCLG provides continuity when city's political will fluctuates (IFP11b, Marc Serra). Limits: vulnerability if Barcelona cuts funding; rejected diversification strategies. PP comparison (IPO4, Carla Bezerra).

### Theme 4: Managed Openness — Including the Non-Democratic
**Where:** Dimensions 2, 3, 5. **Sources:** ITR1, IPO4.

Adrià's philosophy: *"It's better to have one city of a non-democratic country being part of our neighborhood because... with us they're gonna be listening about our narrative"* (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Ethical tensions (Tehran) resolved through distributed decision-making. Spectrum: Decidim (assertive) → OIDP (managed openness) → PP (neutrality) (IPO4, Carla Bezerra).

### Theme 5: The UCLG Embrace — Integration Without Absorption
**Where:** Dimensions 3, 4, 7. **Sources:** ITR1, IFP6, ITR3, IFP11b.

Evolution from bureaucratic shelter (2007) to structural integration (2024) while maintaining separate identity. Dual-coordinator as novel organizational form. Marc Serra's legal rationale (IFP11b, Marc Serra). Fernando's merger caution (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

### Theme 6: The Defensive Turn and Its Limits
**Where:** Dimensions 1, 7. **Sources:** ITR1, IFP6, IPO4.

Shift from improvement to defense discourse is real but may be more rhetorical than substantive, as core activities unchanged. May be narrative frame securing relevance in hostile political environment rather than practice transformation.

## 5. Cross-Case Patterns by Dimension

*Patterns identified through 12 interviews. Tentative where < 3 sources.*

### Pattern 1: Institutional Nesting (Dim 3)
Small networks achieve sustainability/legal credibility by nesting within larger umbrellas. OIDP's trajectory: Barcelona-only → UCLG-bureaucratic (2007) → UCLG-integrated (2024). Marc Serra on legal shelter (IFP11b, Marc Serra). **Status:** DOMINANT. **Sources:** ITR1, IFP6, ITR3, IFP11b.

### Pattern 2: Award as Substitute Core Function (Dim 6)
Name implies function (observation) no longer structurally performed; award takes over, providing symbolic coherence. Adrià confirms (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). **Status:** DOMINANT. **Sources:** ITR1.

### Pattern 3: Defensive Turn (Dim 7)
Rhetorical shift from optimistic improvement to defensive protection, driven by global backsliding. Adrià (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño), Fernando (IFP6, Fernando Pindado), Carla (IPO4, Carla Bezerra). **Status:** DOMINANT for OIDP; EMERGING across corpus. **Sources:** ITR1, IFP6, IPO4.

### Pattern 4: Lean Resilience (Dim 4)
Small budget + institutional nesting = survival through political volatility. Contrasts with PP's diversified fundraising. **Status:** DOMINANT for OIDP; CASE-SPECIFIC. **Sources:** ITR1, IFP6, IFP11b.

### Pattern 5: Managed Openness Spectrum (Dim 5)
Decidim (assertive) → OIDP (managed openness) → PP (neutrality). Tehran case (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño); Carla's comparison (IPO4, Carla Bezerra). **Status:** EMERGING. **Sources:** ITR1, IPO4.

### Pattern 6: Waste of Experience (Dim 2)
Institution-centric networks systematically lose individual knowledge when people transition out. Nanterre (ITR3, Melissa Pomeroy); Fernando's formulation (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). **Status:** TENTATIVE. **Sources:** ITR3, IFP6.

### Chapter X: OIDP — The Observatory That Stopped Observing (And Why It Still Works)

1. **Introduction: The Name That Didn't Change** — From EU project to global network. Why 'Observatory'? Urbal A/B model (ITR3, ITR1). Name persistence as path-dependence. Fernando's critique (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

4. **Lean Resilience** — Budget: Barcelona + EU via UCLG. Why fees rejected (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Small budget as survival strategy (ITR1, IFP11b). PP comparison (IPO4, Carla Bezerra).

5. **The UCLG Embrace** — Bureaucratic shelter (2007) → dual coordination (2024). Legal shelter (IFP11b, Marc Serra). Political rationale: UN access (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Reputational asset (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). Merger debate (IFP6, Fernando Pindado).

6. **Managed Openness** — Tehran case (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Distributed decision-making. Spectrum: Decidim → OIDP → PP (IPO4, Carla Bezerra).

7. **The Defensive Turn** — Narrative shift and limits (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño). Global democratic decline (IFP6, Fernando Pindado). COVID (ITR1, Adrià Duarte Griño).

8. **Conclusion: What OIDP Reveals About Network Evolution** — Six patterns. OIDP as second-order networking. Lessons for networks under democratic backsliding.
