# ASVIS / Ecosistema Futuro — Italian Alliance for Sustainable Development
## Analytical Case Report — Phase 2 (Nets4DEM)

> **Source:** IP05, Luca Miggiano (Manager of Ecosistema Futuro, hosted within ASVIS). Single interview, June 2026.

---

## 1. Case Overview

**What ASVIS/Ecosistema Futuro is:** ASVIS (Alleanza Italiana per lo Sviluppo Sostenibile) is an alliance of ~300 member organizations founded in 2016 by Enrico Giovannini to promote the UN Sustainable Development Goals in Italy. Within ASVIS, **Ecosistema Futuro** (Future Ecosystem) was launched in 2024 as a distinct initiative — not a network, but deliberately an "ecosystem" — to promote long-term thinking, futures literacy, and anticipatory governance in Italy. It coordinates research, museum networks, school programs, training, and a forthcoming National Assembly on the Future.

**Central source:** IP05 (Luca Miggiano), the first and only staff member hired for Ecosistema Futuro, serving as its manager/director.

**What the case is mainly about:** Ecosistema Futuro represents an experiment in organizational form — deliberately choosing "ecosystem" over "network" or "alliance" to signal a more distributed, non-hierarchical mode of collaboration. It illustrates the **strategic use of naming** to shape organizational culture, the **tension between formalization and lightness**, and the **role of international peer networks** (particularly SOIF/SUAF) in legitimizing and inspiring national initiatives.

## 2. Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

### Dimension 1: Organisational Identity — Why "Ecosystem"?

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

*The name as deliberate choice:* Luca explicitly contrasts "ecosystem" with "network" and "alliance": *"Ecosystem, I always say... it should be a step forward, it should be like the mycelium of mushrooms, it should be something... without a center, it should be more scattered, that simply grows"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). The choice reflects an aspiration toward distributed, non-hierarchical organization — though he acknowledges this is aspirational: *"I don't know if we'll succeed because it's very complex"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano).

*Context within ASVIS:* ASVIS itself is a more traditional alliance with a secretariat of 40 people, working groups, and formal membership. Ecosistema Futuro was designed to be lighter: *"There's a central team but it's not a large secretariat, there are two of us, there will soon be three"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). The contrast between parent organization (ASVIS/alliance) and child initiative (ecosystem) is instructive.

*Logo as distributed identity:* A distinctive practice: *"All the participants can use the Ecosistema Futuro logo without asking my permission, while for ASVIS patronage you need permission"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). This open-use logo policy embodies the ecosystem philosophy — participants are co-owners of the identity, not subjects of a central authority.

### Dimension 2: Membership — Organizations Only, But Lightly Governed

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

*Membership structure:* ~100 participating organizations. Only organizations can join, not individuals. Membership is by invitation from the Steering Committee, accepted via email (or formal MoU for public institutions like universities). Acceptance implies approval of two documents: the strategy and the logo usage policy.

*Light governance of membership:* Joining is simple — an email acceptance suffices. For public institutions, formal Memoranda of Understanding are signed (about 35 of 100 participants), which are legally more binding (specifying dispute resolution, delegate responsibilities). But the default is informality.

*Logo usage rules:* The logo policy specifies what the logo CAN and CANNOT be used for: *"It is written that the logo can be used for a whole series of things and it cannot be used for... commercial initiatives, or political initiatives, or initiatives against the Italian constitution"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). The Steering Committee reserves the right to remove logo usage rights — this is the primary sanction mechanism.

*Individual vs. institutional tension:* Luca participates personally in the Network of Institutions and Leaders for Future Generations: *"I am as a person, but I think I'm there because I'm responsible for the future ecosystem"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). He acknowledges that when he changes jobs, his personal membership may end — illustrating the individual-institution paradox identified across the Nets4DEM corpus.

*Gender and generational equity:* When an organization joins, it must name a delegate and an alternate, and *"the alternate must be of the opposite sex"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). The Steering Committee includes 5 individual expert seats selected with gender and generational equity criteria. However, youth representation in governance is acknowledged as weaker: *"We are a little weaker when it comes to young people"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano) — though the National Assembly on the Future project specifically targets under-35s.

*Geographic imbalance:* *"There aren't many organizations from the South among the founding institutions. There are only three"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). The ecosystem is aware of this bias and attempts to compensate through geographically distributed events (Agnone, Catanzaro, Milan, Parma).

### Dimension 3: Governance — Steering Committee + Light Secretariat

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

*Governance structure:* A Steering Committee (~20 people, founding institutions and strategic partners) chaired by Enrico Giovannini makes three types of decisions: strategy, business plan, and membership invitations. Luca is accountable to both the Steering Committee and the ASVIS Secretary General (his line manager).

*The coordinator's role:* Luca describes himself as essentially the manager/project manager: *"I am the first staff, number one staff and therefore I am essentially the manager... who provides energy and creates needs"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). The small team (2 people, soon 3) coordinates a much larger distributed effort through task forces led by participants themselves: *"Our task forces are actually made up of the participants, they also coordinate them"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano).

*Conflict management so far:* No conflicts have emerged. Luca attributes this to the topic's low politicization: *"The topic is still very little politicized about the future"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). The ecosystem's framing — "we create a space to discuss" — has not yet been tested by reputational crises.

### Dimension 4: Resources — ASVIS-Hosted, Mainstream-Funded

**Presence: PARTIALLY PRESENT** (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

*Hosting arrangement:* Ecosistema Futuro is hosted within ASVIS, which provides legal entity status, manages criminal/legal responsibilities, and employs the staff. This hosting model (similar to OIDP's relationship with UCLG) provides institutional shelter without requiring Ecosistema Futuro to incorporate separately.

*Funding:* Not detailed in the interview. The initiative has secured funding for the National Assembly on the Future and other activities, but specific funders, budget size, or diversification strategies are not discussed.

*Mainstream positioning as resource strategy:* Luca explicitly positions Ecosistema Futuro as mainstream (not "new economy" or radical): *"We see that our added value is having a mainstream organization... the goal is to be mainstream, the most reformist of the mainstream"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). This positioning facilitates access to mainstream institutions and funders, though it may limit the initiative's ability to engage with more radical futures discourse.

### Dimension 5: External Relations — SOIF/SUAF as International Peer Network

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

*SOIF/SUAF relationship:* The most significant external relationship. Luca discovered SOIF at the Dubai Future Forum and actively sought connection: *"I want to join, but precisely as a community of practice"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). He praises SOIF's approach: *"They are very open... they essentially offered many opportunities for exchange, for knowledge... they have a collective action approach"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano).

*Nature of the relationship:* Currently informal — no MoU, no formal agreement. Luca proposed one but is ambivalent: *"I wonder how necessary it is to formalize these things, because the good thing about not formalizing it is that there's no secretariat anyway, it's all very light"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). SOIF uses Ecosistema Futuro as a case study; Ecosistema Futuro looks to SOIF as a model and sparring partner.

*International networking as personal initiative:* Luca deliberately carved out time for international networking despite it not being in Ecosistema Futuro's formal outcome framework: *"I don't have to give to my donors, to my board, I don't have to say anything international, but I deliberately carved out space for this thing"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). This illustrates how individual conviction drives network-building beyond formal mandates.

*Other international connections:* Club of Rome, Partners for New Economy, Well-Being Economic Alliance, Zoe Institute, UN Futures Lab — Luca identifies a sub-community of serious futures practitioners within the broader (and sometimes questionable) futures field encountered at the Dubai Future Forum.

*The Dubai Future Forum as filtering space:* Luca describes the Forum as containing *"everything, there are people in the mask, artificial intelligence, techno-solutionists, many charlatans, really many, and then there's a group of people who are very serious"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). The Forum functions as an unstructured space where Ecosistema Futuro can identify and connect with like-minded actors — a filtering mechanism for international alliance-building.

### Dimension 6: Knowledge and Learning

**Presence: PARTIALLY PRESENT** (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

*Knowledge production:* Four areas: research (coordinated by Carraro/Ricci), outreach (Luca De Biase), education/culture (Poli/Pietrabissa), and participation (Giulia Di Donato/Viviana Pinto). Products include Future Briefs, a magazine (Futura Network), a radio program (Oggi Futuro), and scenario work toward "Italy 2050."

*Training:* Courses with SNA (National School of Administration), MIC (Ministry of Culture), and the National Youth Council. The generational impact assessment law was a significant achievement, influenced by Ecosistema Futuro's advocacy.

*Museum network:* 41 museums in a "network of museums of the future" designed to promote futures literacy through experiential learning.

*SOIF as knowledge source:* SOIF's handbook influenced Ecosistema Futuro's approach: *"It was nice because they used this very word, you don't need an alliance... you need an ecosystem. And that had already occurred to us independently"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano) — an example of convergent evolution rather than direct borrowing.

### Dimension 7: Adaptation — The Ecosystem as Experiment

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

*Experimental organizational form:* Ecosistema Futuro is explicitly an experiment: *"It's an experiment that starts from an idea of identity that is, however, very difficult to implement"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). The aspiration toward a truly distributed, non-hierarchical ecosystem is acknowledged as aspirational.

*The National Assembly on the Future as adaptation:* A civic assembly of 100–150 people discussing Italy's future represents an ambitious participatory innovation. A constituent assembly of 52 people (40 under 35, 12 mentors including former prime ministers and constitutional court presidents) is designing the process. This represents the ecosystem's most significant adaptive move — from research and advocacy to direct democratic experimentation.

*Non-politicization as strategic advantage:* The topic of "the future" is not yet politicized in Italy: *"This is a topic that hasn't been politicized yet and so people still use it a bit as common sense"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano). This allows Ecosistema Futuro to build broad coalitions across political divides — the constitutional reform on future generations passed almost unanimously. However, Luca acknowledges uncertainty about whether this non-politicization will persist.

### Dimension 8: Commoning

**Presence: MARGINALLY PRESENT** (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

*Logo as commons identifier:* The open-use logo policy — participants can use it without permission — enacts a commons logic. The logo becomes a shared resource that participants collectively own and deploy.

*Knowledge sharing days:* Instead of frontal presentations, Ecosistema Futuro organizes *"knowledge exchange days"* where participants connect laterally — a practice that mirrors commoning principles of peer-to-peer exchange.

*Website as shared infrastructure:* The website is designed with two sections: collaborative projects and individual network projects, making visible both the collective and the individual contributions.

### Dimension 9: Impacts

**Presence: PARTIALLY PRESENT** (IP05, Luca Miggiano)

*Policy impact:* The generational impact assessment law (passed October–November 2024) is a concrete policy achievement influenced by Ecosistema Futuro's advocacy. The constitutional reform on future generations (2022) was an ASVIS achievement that created the conditions for Ecosistema Futuro.

*Institutional recognition:* High-profile mentors in the constituent assembly (Giuliano Amato, Silvana Sciarra, Mario Monti) signal significant institutional buy-in.

*Media visibility:* Events featured on ANSA (national news agency); radio program on Radio Radicale; museum network with 41 institutions.

*Limitation:* Initiative is only ~1.5 years old. Most impacts are process-level (partnerships formed, events held) rather than outcome-level. The National Assembly on the Future has not yet occurred.

## 3. Cross-Cutting Analytical Themes

### Theme 1: The Ecosystem as Aspirational Form

Ecosistema Futuro's choice of "ecosystem" over "network" or "alliance" reflects an aspiration toward truly distributed, non-hierarchical organization — "like the mycelium of mushrooms" (IP05, Luca Miggiano). But Luca acknowledges the gap between aspiration and reality: the coordinator inevitably becomes a focal point because participants have limited time. The ecosystem is an organizational hypothesis being tested in practice.

### Theme 2: Strategic Non-Politicization

The future is not yet a politicized topic in Italy, allowing Ecosistema Futuro to build coalitions across political divides. The constitutional reform passed almost unanimously. But this strategy carries risk: if the topic becomes politicized, the broad coalition may fracture. Luca is uncertain whether non-politicization is durable: *"I don't know what the answer is"* (IP05, Luca Miggiano).

### Theme 3: International Peer Networks as Legitimacy and Inspiration

SOIF/SUAF functions not as a formal partner but as a peer community — a source of legitimacy, inspiration, and practical models. The relationship remains deliberately informal, and Luca questions whether formalization would add value or create unnecessary bureaucracy. This model of "light international networking" — maintained by individual conviction rather than organizational mandate — may be a pattern among nationally-focused initiatives seeking global connection.

### Theme 4: The Logo as Distributed Governance Tool

The open-use logo policy is a small but significant governance innovation. By allowing participants to use the ecosystem's visual identity without permission, it enacts distributed ownership. The logo becomes a commons — and the Steering Committee's reserved right to withdraw usage is the primary (and so far unused) governance lever.
