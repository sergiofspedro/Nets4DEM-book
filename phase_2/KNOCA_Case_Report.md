# KNOCA — Knowledge Network on Climate Assemblies
## Analytical Case Report — Phase 2 (Nets4DEM)

> **Source:** IFP8, Graham Smith (Chair). Single interview, June 2026.

---

## 1. Case Overview

**What KNOCA is:** The Knowledge Network on Climate Assemblies is an informal, project-based network of individuals — practitioners, public officials, academics, and advocates — focused on improving the practice and impact of climate assemblies. Founded around 2020 with funding from the European Climate Foundation (ECF), it operates **without legal status**, has **no institutional members**, and is coordinated by Professor Graham Smith (University of Westminster). KNOCA runs online events, produces guidance documents, curates a knowledge base (YouTube library, website), and facilitates peer-to-peer connections among those designing, commissioning, and advocating for climate assemblies.

**Distinctive model:** KNOCA represents a deliberately bounded, methodologically focused, individual-based network that chose **self-limitation over growth** — refusing to broaden its thematic scope, refusing to formalize legally, and now deliberately scaling down as the community of practice it helped create has matured. It exemplifies "network effectiveness through self-limitation" (IFP8, Graham Smith).

**Current state:** Activity reducing from 9–10 events/year at its peak to 2–3/year. Knowledge base considered mature. The network may continue at a maintenance level or fade as its community becomes self-sustaining. Graham now spends 1 day/week (down from 3).

## 2. Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

### Dimension 1: Organisational Identity

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IFP8, Graham Smith)

*Name and focus:* KNOCA's name explicitly signals its identity: a knowledge network (not an advocacy organization, not a membership body) focused on a single method (climate assemblies). Graham: *"We haven't broadened... we stayed focused on that. And there's something to be said about... the success of the network has been that we haven't tried to be. We stayed focused"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Not an organization:* KNOCA has no legal status — a deliberate choice: *"We never found it necessary. It was more about... we wanted to keep things really light. We wanted the effort to be about affecting practice and supporting people and less about internal bureaucracy"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Three phases:* (1) **Launch (2020–2022):** High activity, 9–10 events/year, building knowledge base. (2) **Maturity (2022–2024):** Community consolidated, guidance documents produced, impact strategies refined. (3) **Consolidation (2024–present):** Reduced to 2–3 events/year, maintenance mode, knowledge base considered complete.

*Self-assessment:* Graham describes KNOCA as having changed the baseline for climate assemblies: *"Five years ago, people were really obsessed about how do we run a good climate assembly. And now... the starting point now, the knowledge base about what makes a good assembly... is so much higher than it was when we started"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

### Dimension 2: Membership — Individuals Only

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IFP8, Graham Smith)

*Individual-based by design:* Graham explicitly rejected organizational membership: *"I said, this is a really bad idea because I know that Extinction Rebellion Bologna will want to be a member. And as soon as Extinction Rebellion Bologna becomes a member... the City of Milan might want to remove it. So I said, right from the start, I don't want any institutional memberships. We are a network of people"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Community size:* ~1000 newsletter subscribers, 40–120 per event, with ~10–20 regulars. Different events attract different audiences depending on the topic. *"They're not always the same people"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*This choice enables managed political diversity:* On the same call, *"we will have a public official, a conservative public official... and someone from Extinction Rebellion, and they will be having a conversation about how best to facilitate"* (IFP8, Graham Smith). Individual membership depoliticizes participation by focusing on shared practice rather than organizational identity.

*Geographic focus:* European, by funder mandate: *"We were established as a network for European practitioners... I don't have the experience of working in the global South. So if we were gonna do that work, we'd have to change KNOCA into something"* (IFP8, Graham Smith). Materials are freely available globally, but the network does not actively serve non-European contexts.

### Dimension 3: Governance — Informal and Person-Centered

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IFP8, Graham Smith)

*No formal governance:* Graham: *"A lot of it has really depended on me playing that role... If I'm wrong, then we're kind of screwed. Not having a larger structure at the center has allowed us to move quickly... but it does come with the price of people's judgments about what the right next step is"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Decision-making:* Decisions about scope, events, and strategy were made by Graham in consultation with ECF funders and key community members. No formal democratic processes.

*The individual-as-institution:* Graham's scholarly reputation (established before KNOCA) and his position (university salary meant he didn't need to generate income from the network) were crucial contingencies: *"I was a slightly unusual choice... I was kind of quite established in the field already and I was recognized by radicals and recognized by policy people as helpful. And I was somehow able to keep that"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

### Dimension 4: Resources — Donor-Dependent and Light

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IFP8, Graham Smith)

*Single funder model:* KNOCA was entirely funded by the European Climate Foundation for its first 5 years. ECF's decision to shut down its governance program created a funding reduction.

*No diversification strategy:* The network was always conceived as temporary: *"Initially we thought this project would be like one or two years max. So I'm quite surprised to still be doing this now"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Current financial situation:* ECF has moved KNOCA to another part of the institution with less money. Simultaneously, *"there is less money coming in around climate and democracy"* (IFP8, Graham Smith). The network is deliberately downsizing rather than seeking new funders.

*Time investment:* At its peak, Graham spent 3 days/week on KNOCA; now 1 day/week.

### Dimension 5: External Relations

**Presence: PARTIALLY PRESENT** (IFP8, Graham Smith)

*Key partnerships:* **FIDE** (co-organized two-day training workshops for assembly practitioners); **Democracy R&D** and **International IDEA** (global South adaptation); **Extinction Rebellion** (informal relationship — Graham was consulted in XR's early days and discovered later that *"the people at the centre were reading a lot of my work"* (IFP8, Graham Smith)).

*Spillover effects — UniDeLib:* *"There's a network now of universities that run climate assemblies... called UniDeLib... They got their idea of doing that from KNOCA. They looked at KNOCA and went, oh, we could run a small network on universities assembly"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*The Turkey/Izmir case:* A Turkish practitioner discovered assemblies through KNOCA's global assembly, received training (with FIDE), used KNOCA materials to legitimize the approach with Turkish officials, and successfully ran Turkey's first climate assembly. Graham served on his advisory board and arranged a study visit to London. *"If KNOCA hadn't been there, it's not clear that he would have been able to actually move from the idea... to actually running an assembly"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

### Dimension 6: Knowledge and Learning

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IFP8, Graham Smith)

*Knowledge production:* Guidance documents (permanent assemblies, knowledge curation, evidence), online events, YouTube library. *"We've got a huge library of events... our YouTube channel is used a lot by people who matter"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Changing the baseline:* Graham's strongest impact claim: *"We changed the baseline for assemblies... The expectations about what do we do afterwards? How can we involve assembly members as actors after the assembly? These things, we've thought a lot about that"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Open knowledge:* All materials are freely available: *"Everything gets borrowed off our website, we don't care"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Personal learning:* *"Every time we would do something, I would learn something new and I would hear something inspiring. That's the only reason... How could I do this for five years?"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

### Dimension 7: Adaptation — Deliberate Downsizing

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IFP8, Graham Smith)

*Strategic reduction as success:* *"We've done a lot of the work we feel we need to do... The community of practice is more mature and people know each other because of the role that KNOCA played. People are having those conversations direct with each other rather than having to be brokered by KNOCA"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Fading by design:* The network was never meant to be permanent: *"Part of me all the time was thinking, why am I spending all my time focusing on climate assemblies on their own?"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Adaptation to unexpected evolution:* Assemblies evolved beyond initial imagination — civil society-commissioned, permanent, university-based, children's assemblies. KNOCA stayed methodologically focused while remaining open to these developments: *"We are open to being surprised"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Radical engagement:* KNOCA maintained dialogue with Extinction Rebellion and other radical actors. Graham analyzes XR's strategic misstep: *"Local activists made a mistake because they forgot that actually the assembly demand of Extinction Rebellion was tied to an initial political commitment to... 2035 decarbonisation"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

### Dimension 8: Commoning

**Presence: MARGINALLY PRESENT** (IFP8, Graham Smith)

*Knowledge commons:* All materials open access. The network created a shared knowledge base practitioners worldwide draw upon without membership or payment.

*Community of practice as commons:* Value is created collectively: *"The value of the network is only the value of those people who actually turn up and make it valuable. If that wasn't working, then no one would come to our events"* (IFP8, Graham Smith). The network is *"utterly involuntary"* — it exists only through voluntary contribution.

### Dimension 9: Impacts

**Presence: PARTIALLY PRESENT** (IFP8, Graham Smith)

*Knowledge baseline shift:* The most significant impact — changing practitioner expectations about quality (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Case-level impact:* Turkey/Izmir assembly directly enabled (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Methodological influence:* Shifted focus from assembly mechanics to embedding assemblies in governance — the "assemblage" approach (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Inspiration effect:* UniDeLib (university climate assembly network) modeled on KNOCA (IFP8, Graham Smith).

*Limitation:* No systematic impact evaluation. Claims based on practitioner testimony.

## 3. Cross-Cutting Analytical Themes

### Theme 1: Effectiveness Through Self-Limitation

KNOCA's success derives from refusing to grow or broaden. Graham: *"Once you start opening up beyond one method, it's really hard to hold the network together. Everyone gets really confused"* (IFP8, Graham Smith). This contrasts with the dominant impulse to expand. When ECF considered broadening KNOCA's scope, the decision was: *"If ECF was gonna support a wider thinking about participatory climate governance, KNOCA should be part of it, but it shouldn't be the vehicle of it"* (IFP8, Graham Smith). KNOCA demonstrates that network effectiveness can come from knowing what you are NOT — and protecting that boundary.

### Theme 2: The Individual-as-Network

KNOCA's entire functioning depended on one person. Graham acknowledges: *"I might be wrong. And if I'm wrong, then we're kind of screwed"* (IFP8, Graham Smith). This vulnerability is the price of agility. The network had no institutional memory beyond Graham; its legacy is embedded in the community of practice and knowledge base it created. The model worked because of specific contingencies — Graham's academic wage, established reputation, and personal credibility across political divides — that would be difficult to replicate.

### Theme 3: Temporary by Design

Unlike most networks that seek permanence, KNOCA was conceived as temporary. Its current downsizing is success, not failure — the community it built can now function without it. This represents a model of network-as-catalyst: create the conditions for a community of practice, then fade. *"If we feel that there is suddenly need for us, we can reappear and do more work"* (IFP8, Graham Smith).

### Theme 4: Individual Membership as Political Neutralizer

By admitting only individuals, not organizations, KNOCA created a space where Extinction Rebellion activists and conservative public officials could discuss facilitation techniques together. This depoliticized participation around shared practice. The key insight: *"We are a network of people who are interested in asking the question of how can we make assemblies have more impact?"* (IFP8, Graham Smith) — the shared question, not shared ideology, is the glue.
