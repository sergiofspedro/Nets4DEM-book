# PARES.it — Participatory Democracy Consultancy within the Decidim Ecosystem
## Analytical Case Report — Phase 2 (Nets4DEM)

> **Source:** IPO8, Giulia Bertone (enterprise PARES, Decidim partner in Italy). Single interview, June 2026.

---

## 1. Case Overview

**What PARES.it is:** PARES is an Italian consultancy/enterprise specializing in participatory processes using the Decidim open-source platform. It operates as an official partner of the Decidim Association, providing Decidim installations, configuration, training, and process support primarily to Italian municipalities. PARES represents the **commercial/consultancy layer** of the Decidim ecosystem — a small enterprise that bridges between the open-source software community, public administrations, and citizens.

**Central source:** IPO8 (Giulia Bertone). Single interview.

**What the case is mainly about:** PARES illustrates the role of **commercial intermediaries in open-source ecosystems** — enterprises that translate community-developed technology into usable services for public administrations while feeding back local needs and experiences into the community. The case reveals the **gap between technological potential and political will**, the **multi-tenant governance challenge**, and the **frustration of working within a community where development priorities are set by developers for developers**.

## 2. Dimension-by-Dimension Analysis

### Dimension 1: Organisational Identity — Enterprise Within an Ecosystem

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)

*Position within the Decidim ecosystem:* PARES is not a network itself but a node within the MetaDecidim community — an enterprise that both consumes and contributes to the open-source ecosystem. Giulia describes her initial encounter with Decidim: *"When I started to explore the potential of Decidim better, I fell in love... having access to it at a basically acceptable cost... with all the trappings we know about attention to deliberative quality"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone).

*The community as value:* *"The value of the community that exists around it... is extremely important for us in the sense that you feel like you're not alone in having identified the need for that feature. You see it emerge in discussions"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). The community provides both practical resources (shared promotional materials, technical support) and psychological reassurance.

*Relationship with the Decidim Association:* PARES became an official partner in January 2026: *"We've seen that this sharing process is also real... they shared the drive with us where the open files were"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). Partnership provides access to materials, translation support, and networking opportunities at events like Decidim Fest.

### Dimension 2: Membership/Community Participation

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)

*Asymmetric participation in community governance:* Giulia acknowledges that PARES does not actively participate in MetaDecidim's development governance: *"I see things happening, I see parties being held, I see online meetings being held to discuss the community in MetaDecidim, but unfortunately I haven't had time to actively participate yet"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). She follows development discussions but doesn't contribute to priority-setting. The reason is practical: *"I am physically in a condition where I cannot find the time"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone).

*The accessibility question:* Giulia emphasizes that participation is not overly demanding — *"I realized that very little was needed"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone) — but even minimal time investment is challenging for small enterprises. This creates a structural bias: those with more resources (larger enterprises, well-funded institutions) have disproportionate influence on community governance.

*Welcoming culture:* At Decidim Fest in Barcelona (September 2025), Giulia experienced openness despite being new: *"I was a bit worried about entering a context of unfamiliar people who had perhaps been there for centuries... there was a great openness, even to new people... a great willingness and welcome"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). The community's culture appears genuinely inclusive, even if practical participation barriers remain.

### Dimension 3: Governance — Community-Driven Development with Gaps

**Presence: PARTIALLY PRESENT** (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)

*Community governance structure:* MetaDecidim has forums for discussing features and development priorities, with an active proposal collection process. Giulia observes this but cannot assess whether participation is substantive or performative: *"I can't tell you whether this participation in the design and governance of the software project is real or just a facade"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). This uncertainty itself is significant — peripheral community members lack visibility into whether governance processes are genuinely democratic.

*Developer-centric governance:* An important critique emerges implicitly. The community that governs Decidim's development is primarily developers: *"To be part of MetaDecidim, you can be both as an individual or as an enterprise that is a developer"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). Governments and end-users have no formal seats — though a Council of Governments was recently created, it remains consultative. This developer-centricity shapes what features get prioritized.

*The multi-tenant challenge:* A recurring theme is the **multi-tenant function** — the ability for a regional authority to host Decidim for multiple small municipalities, managing the technical complexity centrally. Catalonia has implemented this (via AOC); Italy has not. Giulia has repeatedly advocated for it with Emilia-Romagna region: *"I really don't understand if, from a political point of view... what the reason is that Emilia Romagna can't make the leap on this issue"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). The interviewer notes: *"Multitenant governance has the ability to guarantee both centralization and autonomy... you can collect all the data... grant complete autonomy to the peripheries, but maintain a constant center"* (IPO8, interviewer). The absence of multi-tenant governance in Italy represents a structural barrier to Decidim adoption by small municipalities.

### Dimension 4: Resources — The Cost Barrier

**Presence: STRONGLY PRESENT** (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)

*The cost problem:* PARES started with "garage" Decidim installations for very small municipalities, but as needs grew, they required professional hosting: *"The need arose to rely on even more robust solutions from a security standpoint... it also comes at a cost... we finally found ourselves unable to provide it to them at prices they can afford"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). The cost of professional Decidim deployment is a barrier for small municipalities — the very entities that most need accessible participation tools.

*The multi-tenant solution:* A multi-tenant architecture (one installation serving many municipalities) could dramatically reduce per-municipality costs. A municipality of Reggio Emilia asked PARES: *"When is it coming? Because we need this stuff. We can't use the space currently guaranteed by the regional platform"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). The demand exists; the political will to implement multi-tenant governance does not.

*The "tool-first" mentality of administrations:* Giulia describes administrations that acquire the platform but don't invest in participatory culture: *"They often ask us, 'Do we want the platform, do we want to set it up?' It's a bit like the idea that the tool alone — boom! — will create who knows what"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). The tool is acquired without the cultural infrastructure to use it effectively.

*Parma as success case:* The Municipality of Parma provides a counter-example. PARES supported Parma in rewriting its participation regulations and integrating Decidim with neighborhood workshops (formerly citizen committees): *"A mechanism has been triggered for these workshops to function, one that communicates closely with the platform... It has contributed not only to transparency, but also to ordering communication flows"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). The platform became infrastructure for a functioning participatory process, not just a tool.

### Dimension 5: External Relations — Between Community and Market

**Presence: PARTIALLY PRESENT** (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)

*Relationship with the Decidim community:* PARES is an official partner and consumer of community resources, but a limited contributor to community governance. The relationship is asymmetrical: PARES benefits from the community's knowledge and legitimacy, but its capacity to shape community priorities is limited by time and resources.

*Relationship with other Decidim enterprises:* PARES collaborates with Oktri (the Swiss Decidim consultancy working in Tanzania, Mozambique, and Mali): *"We also have a relationship with them... they're the ones who provide us with our Decidim gym that we use"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). These enterprise-to-enterprise connections form a sub-network within the broader Decidim ecosystem.

*National-level engagement:* PARES worked with Formez (Italian government agency) on a national Decidim deployment for the Department of Civil Service. Giulia describes this experience as disappointing: *"There was a great idea there to try to bring together a few experiences, communities, and technology use, but it fizzled out... there was a lot of celebration when 50 questionnaires were filled out nationwide, but what are we talking about?"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). National-level engagement revealed the gap between technological potential and administrative reality: *"Inside there, with the rubber walls... technology is the least of the problems"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone).

*Competition/coopetition:* Giulia briefly mentions evaluating alternatives (Consul) but chose Decidim because of the community, the Italian movement around open-source computing, and the software's quality. The competitive landscape includes other platforms and consultancies, but Decidim's community dimension was decisive.

### Dimension 6: Knowledge and Learning — Training as Translation

**Presence: PARTIALLY PRESENT** (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)

*Training as cultural translation:* PARES includes training in its service packages, attempting to bridge the gap between technological capability and administrative culture: *"In the package we usually sell to public administrations... we always include a component... of training and awareness-raising at various levels"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). Training includes international examples (especially Spanish) to show what's possible.

*The limits of consultant-led training:* The interviewer raises a critical point: *"Politics only evolves when other politicians encourage it. It's very difficult for a consultant to evolve, because the impression they might get is that you're continuing to sell a product"* (IPO8, interviewer). This structural limitation means that consultant-led training may be insufficient to shift political culture — peer-to-peer learning among politicians may be necessary.

*Decidim Fest as learning space:* The annual Decidim Fest provides exposure to diverse experiences: *"There was a wide array of different experiences, and I found it incredibly enriching and inspiring... so much so that the Municipality of Parma had to come too, to learn what can be done"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). These events function as knowledge diffusion mechanisms across the ecosystem.

### Dimension 7: Adaptation — Frustration and Persistence

**Presence: PARTIALLY PRESENT** (IPO8, Giulia Bertone)

*Working within constraints:* PARES operates within significant constraints — cost barriers for small municipalities, limited political will, slow-moving public administrations, and a developer-centric community governance. Giulia's posture is one of persistent advocacy within these constraints: multiple attempts to push multi-tenant solutions in Emilia-Romagna, despite repeated rejection.

*The platform's evolution:* Giulia acknowledges Decidim's ongoing improvement: *"At least lately there have been leaps and bounds... even from a design perspective, it seems to me that we're really keeping pace"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). The software evolves, but adoption lags.

*The user experience critique:* A recurring frustration: *"People say the platform, even though they've improved it, isn't sexy"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). The platform's complexity — a consequence of its comprehensive feature set — creates usability barriers, especially for citizens with limited digital literacy. The SPID/CIE authentication requirement cuts out young people who may not have these credentials.

## 3. Cross-Cutting Analytical Themes

### Theme 1: The Intermediary as Translator

PARES occupies a crucial intermediary position — translating between the developer-centric Decidim community and public administrations that need usable, affordable participation tools. This translation work includes technical configuration, training, cultural advocacy, and feedback to the community. The intermediary role is essential for open-source ecosystems to reach non-technical users, but it comes with structural limitations (limited time for community governance, dependency on political will).

### Theme 2: The Multi-Tenant Governance Gap

The absence of multi-tenant infrastructure in Italy is the single greatest barrier to Decidim adoption by small municipalities. A multi-tenant solution would enable regional authorities to host Decidim centrally while granting autonomy to individual municipalities — dramatically reducing costs and technical barriers. The Catalonia model (via AOC) demonstrates feasibility; the Italian failure to replicate it reveals the gap between technical possibility and political implementation.

### Theme 3: The Tool-Culture Gap

Administrations acquire the platform but not the participatory culture to use it. *"If you imagine and understand participation only as a questionnaire about a policy you're developing, then you don't even feel the need to look for tools"* (IPO8, Giulia Bertone). The gap between technological capability and administrative imagination is a structural problem that no single intermediary can solve alone.

### Theme 4: Developer-Centric Governance and Its Discontents

The MetaDecidim community's governance is shaped by those with time and technical expertise to participate — primarily developers and larger enterprises. Small consultancies like PARES, and especially the public administrations they serve, have limited voice in setting development priorities. This creates a potential democratic deficit in an ecosystem whose stated values include participation and inclusion.
